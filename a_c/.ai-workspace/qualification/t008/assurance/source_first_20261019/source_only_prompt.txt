563:    closed results or declared translations; and
564: 3. authorised action selection: it assigns priority, determines the effect
565:    on the current promotion boundary, applies an existing disposition, and
566:    issues only the next already-authorised activation or action.

572: grant (P6, RF-002).

115: ### Transitions
116: 
117: Construction moves work from a current state towards a destination
118: (P0, P2). The current state is the material state of the workspace and
119: its authoritative inputs, together with what is justifiably known about
120: that state, with the unknowns explicit. The work state of §5 records it:
121: the workspace, the governing basis, and the closed results and judgments
122: that still apply, with the residuals and the uncertainty over external
123: effects as the explicit unknowns. An actor's context is an account of the
124: current state, not the state itself
125: ([§5](#the-workspace-and-an-actors-account)) (P3, P5).
126: 
127: The destination is defined by constraints: the selected outcome with its
128: acceptance conditions (Outcome U, [FOUNDATIONS §2](FOUNDATIONS.md#2-ontology))
129: and the governing obligations the work must keep. It names no single
130: state: its acceptance conditions admit several realised states. Its
131: owning authority defines it (P2, P6).
132: 
133: A transition is a bounded change from the current state to a next state,
134: carried out by one step. Where the work exceeds one activation's capacity,
135: the outer transformation towards the destination is refined into a series
136: of bounded transitions under §4 Refinement, each carried out by its own
137: step; work that fits one activation is a single transition. Each
138: transition binds the obligations it must preserve: the outer obligations
139: of the destination and of the governing relations it affects, as far as
140: they apply to it. A step's intake binds the current state, the
141: destination and the obligations: the governed outcome names the
142: destination, or the prerequisite towards it, that the step serves; the
143: exact subject and basis identify the current state it starts from; and
144: the affected relation set with its governing invariants holds the
145: obligations it preserves (Intake below; P0, P4a).
146: Transitions compose only as §4 Refinement permits.
147: 
148: Discovery. Construction, observation and evaluation change the current
149: state and what is known about it. A new observation may add obligations,
150: which join the known work, or invalidate a claim, which is re-established
151: or withdrawn before anything relies on it (§7). Discovery can enlarge the
152: known work while knowledge improves (P5, P4b).
153: 
154: Open work outside attention. An obligation outside a frame's current
155: attention stays open. A bounded view disposes of none of the work it
156: excludes, and the next decision keeps its material dependencies on that
157: work (P4b, P6).
158: 
159: Destination change. An authorised change may revise the destination. The
160: change follows its owning authority and re-enters at the owning relation
161: (§4). A revised destination does not establish that the previous
162: destination was reached, and a candidate that satisfies the revised
163: destination is no evidence that it satisfies the previous one (P2, P6).
164: 
165: Completion. Work is complete only when sufficient evidence shows that the
166: current state satisfies the applicable destination and its acceptance
167: conditions; accepting that claim is the decision owner's disposition
168: (§3 Disposition). An empty list of known work, a count of closed or
169: remaining items, or the absence of a recorded counterexample does not
170: establish completion (P6;
171: [RF-015](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#rf-015-residual-uncertainty)).
172: 
173: Stops. Work that cannot continue ends in an explicit stop that names its
174: cause: a step that cannot deliver its output stops with a value of
175: construction availability other than `candidate_ready`, and a declared
176: ambiguity that halts the work or exceeds the step's authority stops it
177: with `blocked` or `escalated` (§3). A refused admission or activation
178: stops the work without creating a step (§3 Admission and activation).
179: The work state (§5) retains the unresolved outcome, the residuals and the
180: obligations still owed. A stop reaches no destination and discards none
181: of these (P4b, P5).
182: 
183: Progress. Progress is judged against the selected outcome and the
184: evidence for it, as §5 defines progress, not by the number of remaining
185: items alone: discovery
186: can raise that number while the work moves towards the destination (P2).
187: 
188: Purpose and material relation: finite context and attention (P0) mean
189: that an outcome beyond one activation is reached through bounded steps.
190: Binding the destination and the preserved obligations at each transition
191: keeps the outer contract through that decomposition. Keeping completion,
192: destination change and stops apart prevents a count, an exhausted list or
193: a revised destination from standing in for evidence that the applicable
194: destination is satisfied (P2, P6). The state, the destination and the
195: obligations are those that the intake and the work state already bind; no
196: further carrier is required. This account is new in this corpus.

198: ### Construction relation
199: 
200: This subsection states the construction relation for this corpus: how a
201: selected construction binds its current state and destination, chooses
202: its work, composes its steps, corrects itself and claims completion. It
203: is law carried from the common construction requirement and the universal
204: construction model of the Product that owns this corpus, restated here at
205: its owner. ACTIVATION §2 to §7, EVALUATION §1, SPEC_METHOD §4 and §8,
206: EXECUTIVE_BASELINE, ODD and the other consumers named in the design
207: apply it; none restates it (P6).
208: 
209: Binding A and Z. A construction binds A, the current state of Transitions
210: above with its account, and Z, the destination as a predicate over
211: realised states that includes the acceptance conditions of the selected
212: outcome. The account of A describes the material workspace state, the
213: relevant external observations acquired through the interfaces that own
214: them, what is justifiably known and the explicit unknowns; an actor's
215: acquired account can be incomplete or stale, and the workspace, not the
216: account, is the subject (§5). Several realised states may satisfy Z. The
217: governing specification, the selected WHAT across the Intent, Product and
218: Specification of the Product that owns the work, supplies Z; its
219: descriptions refine Z. The applicable declared requirements and the declared calculi of the
220: tenant's binding ([SPEC_METHOD §11](SPEC_METHOD.md#11-build-tenant-binding))
221: govern which transformations and compositions are lawful; the calculi
222: select no outcome. A
223: lawful construction can remain incomplete or fail to reach Z, and
224: lawfulness establishes neither delivery nor optimality. A proof about the
225: outcome that rests on premises retains those premises and its scope. A
226: selected construction may need to discover its path while performing and
227: evaluating work (P2, P3, P6).
228: 
229: Three accounts of the subject, distinct from the accounts of a
230: construction ([FOUNDATIONS §2](FOUNDATIONS.md#accounts-of-a-construction)).
231: The specification is the internal reference model of the required
232: conditions. Observations describe what does hold. A causal or
233: predictive model explains how an action may change the state. These
234: accounts have different purposes and can be incomplete or wrong in
235: different ways; a claim about one is never taken for a claim about
236: another, and the applicability and adequacy of a predictive model are
237: claims of their own (P1).
238: 
239: Strategy. A strategy arranges admissible construction and assurance to
240: serve Z within the selected resource envelope. Its material choices
241: account for context, attention, time, effort, computation, communication
242: and the other relevant resources, together with feedback delay,
243: uncertainty, integration exposure and rework; planning, checking and
244: coordination consume resources too. Measures that justify a choice state
245: their units, boundaries and composition rules, and keep estimates,
246: assumptions and observations distinguishable. Several objectives can
247: leave alternatives incomparable; the owning criteria decide the tradeoff.
248: Steel thread, phased (waterfall) delivery and iterative delivery are
249: possible strategies, each with its own costs, feedback delays, integration risks and rework.
250: The selection belongs to the realisation and to the actor exercising the
251: admitted work authority; its material rationale is stated in existing
252: design or work records, and a claim that one strategy is superior states
253: the objectives, comparison and assumptions it depends on. No exhaustive
254: optimisation, universal numerical budget, scalar cost measure or preferred
255: strategy is imposed by this relation or its consumers (P2, P5, P6).
256: Context bounds what one activation can hold and attention what it can
257: use; the frame selects the material and the task contract of one step,
258: and the destination stays Z across the activations that reach it
259: ([§2](#2-context-construction-under-an-envelope)).
260: 
261: Composition boundaries. A composition boundary is the boundary between
262: successive steps, where one step's output becomes the next step's
263: starting state. It is distinct from a categorical join, which is a map
264: between categories ([FOUNDATIONS §4](FOUNDATIONS.md#4-algebra)). Steps
265: compose through compatible preconditions, effects, results and authority,
266: and each boundary preserves the applicable outer obligations. Refinement
267: can add detail and construction can change state within the permitted
268: variation (§4). Decomposition, delegation, concurrent work and recursive
269: application retain the affected obligations; independent actions may
270: proceed concurrently where their dependencies, effects and authority
271: permit. Local success supplies end-to-end success only when the required
272: coverage, composition compatibility and outcome evidence hold; the
273: obligations a chain of steps must discharge are
274: [FOUNDATIONS §4 Preservation through construction](FOUNDATIONS.md#preservation-through-construction).
275: A categorical claim about a construction meets the demonstration of
276: FOUNDATIONS §4 (P4a, P1, P6).
277: 
278: Feedback and diagnosis. Feedback observes the current subject, compares
279: it with the governing conditions, diagnoses a material discrepancy,
280: selects an authorised response, acts and observes the result. Homeostasis
281: means correction towards the required conditions as disturbances and new
282: observations arise; it guarantees neither convergence nor the adequacy of
283: those conditions. For work management a bug is a category of
284: feature/change work: an observed or suspected discrepancy handled through
285: the same intake and delivery mechanism
286: ([SPEC_METHOD §4 Universal intake triage](SPEC_METHOD.md#universal-intake-triage)).
287: Diagnosis distinguishes four causes: a defective realisation, an
288: inadequate governing requirement or design, an inaccurate predictive
289: model, and insufficient observation. A prediction error alone does not
290: decide whether the required outcome holds. A revised model does not erase
291: the counterexample or by itself repair the realisation. Diagnosis preserves unresolved
292: alternatives and routes each supported repair to the owner of its cause;
293: the intake label supplies no repair class and no permission. Severity
294: describes consequence; priority orders the response under the selected
295: outcome, dependencies, resources and authority. A bug list projects
296: existing work state and creates no separate delivery or authority
297: mechanism (P1, P2, P5, P6).
298: 
299: Preservation, progress and completion. Recursive and repeated work
300: distinguish four claims: preservation, that an invariant or obligation is
301: kept through the steps; progress, that the work moves towards Z on
302: evidence; termination; and outcome satisfaction. A formal induction or
303: termination claim supplies its premises and argument; no universal
304: termination proof is required before bounded work can begin, and where
305: such a proof is absent the claim stays within the available evidence.
306: Continuing work has a supported purpose under the selected strategy,
307: which can include reducing uncertainty or testing a bounded hypothesis.
308: Discovery may enlarge the known work (Transitions above), and repeated
309: activity alone establishes no progress. Control handles failure,
310: unavailable information, uncertain effects and exhausted resources
311: through the continuation, repair, recovery, re-entry, yield or stop that
312: §3, §5 and §6 name. Resumption reacquires current reality and retains
313: valid progress; unknown effects are resolved sufficiently before the next
314: dependent action (§5). Ongoing maintenance can continue under its mandate; a
315: delivery claim needs its completion condition. A resource limit can end
316: an attempt with an unresolved outcome; it establishes neither achievement
317: nor impossibility (P5, P2, P6).
318: 
319: Evaluation and completion. Evaluation uses the core result algebra
320: ([FOUNDATIONS §4 Results](FOUNDATIONS.md#results)), preserving failure,
321: missing coverage and uncertainty. Formal proof, mechanical checking,
322: testing and observed use establish their respective claims on their
323: stated subjects, assumptions and boundaries
324: ([FOUNDATIONS §4 Assertions and observation](FOUNDATIONS.md#assertions-and-observation));
325: an unavailable proof remains
326: an explicit obligation where the governing conditions require that proof.
327: Producing a candidate, proving a property and accepting delivery remain
328: distinct acts ([EVALUATION §5](EVALUATION.md#5-acceptance)). Completion
329: requires sufficient evidence against Z, including its acceptance
330: conditions; exhaustion or an unresolved attempt proves neither completion
331: nor impossibility (P5, P6). A change to Z follows its owning authority and
332: the re-entry rules of Transitions (Destination change); it establishes
333: nothing about whether the former Z was reached, and the original
334: counterexample stands.

338: actor enters construction, triage, recovery, assurance and release, and
339: state no second relation. Domain strategy, actor configuration, execution
340: mechanisms and additional assurance policies retain their owners:

343: evidence carriers hold the necessary state ([TICKETS](TICKETS.md); the
344: closed results of §7); the relation adds no independent ledger, required
345: role sequence or orchestration runtime. It establishes no general solver,
346: no fixed organisational structure, no guaranteed convergence and no
347: theorem of categorical universality (P6, P2). Purpose and material
348: relation: bounded activity can consume resources without delivering the
349: intended outcome, and isolated checks can conceal a failed composition;

351: features and defects alike remain governed by their end-to-end
352: obligations (P2, P5, P6).

409: Drafting a step's contract and admitting it are distinct relations, even
410: inside one invocation that drafts, validates, admits and executes; no
411: separate turn, carrier or approval ceremony is implied by the distinction.
412: A contract carries at minimum the states drafted, admitted, rejected and
413: superseded ([TICKETS §5](TICKETS.md#execution-contract-admission)).
414: Execution begins only after deterministic admission or an
415: explicit human override of the exact contract. The drafting actor never
416: infers admission from its own draft, from the presence of a carrier
417: (ticket, sprint entry, work contract), from lane placement or from an
418: `active` label. The admitted result identifies the admission mechanism and
419: its authority, the exact contract identity or digest, the decision and the
420: evidence, including any human override; absent those coordinates the
421: contract stays drafted or rejected, and prose asserting admission is not
422: admission evidence (P6; the actor cannot self-certify its own execution
423: basis). Admission validates the contract against its cited outcome,
424: prerequisite or experiment basis; it does not admit that basis, and
425: drafting, admitting or retaining a contract creates or renews no authority
426: its owner has not admitted (P6, P2;
427: [FOUNDATIONS §2 Authority kinds](FOUNDATIONS.md#authority-kinds), What
428: creates no authority). Only an admitted contract activates a step: no
429: result, child completion, tool output, model response, shared actor or
430: lateral handoff activates another step or authorises continuation by
431: itself, and a profile that routes results names its instances of this
432: rule. Purpose and material relation: a profile and a template each stated
433: this list for one configuration, so the generic rule had no owner (P6).
434: The admitted contract names one
435: durable result and evidence surface; every result, withheld closure and
436: residual obligation is recorded there before return, a conversation return
437: alone is not durable evidence, and if no such surface is authorised and
438: available, admission refuses (P4b). Absent or rejected admission returns
439: the missing work authority without execution, as `admission_refused` with
440: its cause ([§3 Admission and activation](#admission-and-activation)).
441: Provenance:
442: [work-carrier-to-execution rule](stdo://releases/v2.5.1-rc.2/standards/TICKET_METHOD.md#work-carrier-to-execution-rule)
443: and
444: [execution contract admission](stdo://releases/v2.5.1-rc.2/standards/TICKET_METHOD.md#execution-contract-admission);
445: the selection of the carrier (ticket, sprint entry, run-scoped contract)
446: is tier-2 law in the [TICKETS](TICKETS.md) member, which disposes those rows.

448: ### Applicability
449: 
450: The owning evaluation and scope declarations determine which frame
451: obligations apply to the exact subject. Their declared predicates are
452: evaluated from sufficient basis-bound facts: a true predicate requires the
453: applicable evaluation and its result; a false predicate excludes only that
454: obligation, under its cited authority; a missing or ambiguous premise
455: leaves applicability unresolved, not false. A supported inapplicability
456: of a conditional obligation is recorded as such, apart from any result
457: value, and waives no criterion of the member's acceptance (P5, P6; new
458: law carried at iteration 4 from the common construction contract's
459: conformance case on inapplicability). A frame name, an available
460: tool or a listed family triggers no activation by itself. Where
461: applicability needs residual semantic judgment, that judgment and its
462: evidence are retained as a judgment, never represented as a computed fact
463: (P6, P1). A required result that is absent, stale, bound to another subject
464: or basis, or incapable of establishing its claim cannot satisfy a dependent
465: conjunction ([FOUNDATIONS §4 Conjunction of results](FOUNDATIONS.md#conjunction-of-results));
466: unresolved applicability withholds the dependent claim until
467: sufficient evidence or the owning decision resolves it; other applicable
468: constraints stay in force even if only one evaluation detects them (P4b,
469: P5). Qualification of an applicability rule distinguishes true, false and
470: unknown premises, required results that are missing or bound to the wrong
471: subject, and a sufficient positive result, under a finite declared fixture;
472: a rule that always skips or always activates cannot satisfy those distinct
473: expected outcomes (EVALUATION §7; rc.2 RF
474: #applicability-and-required-results).
475: 
476: Exclusion and the core. Deciding applicability is an evaluation of the
477: predicate "the obligation applies to the exact subject", and its result is
478: a core value of [FOUNDATIONS §4 Results](FOUNDATIONS.md#results): a true
479: predicate is `satisfied`; a false predicate is `falsified`, the
480: basis-bound fact that makes it false being the counterexample; a missing
481: or ambiguous premise is `indeterminate`, or `out_of_frame` where the
482: premise needs a relation the frame does not declare, and never
483: `falsified`. An exclusion is the state of the excluded obligation after a
484: `falsified` applicability result: it is no result, the obligation's own
485: evaluation is not performed and maps to no core value, and the obligation
486: leaves the required coverage of a dependent conjunction under the cited
487: authority of that applicability result. An obligation whose applicability is
488: unresolved stays in that required coverage. A predicate that its owner
489: declares as a disjunction that includes non-application, such as "meets
490: the axiom or does not apply", may be `satisfied` because the obligation
491: does not apply; that `satisfied` is for the declared disjunction only and
492: never counts toward the predicate that the obligation holds
493: ([FOUNDATIONS §4 Conjunction of results](FOUNDATIONS.md#conjunction-of-results),
494: Predicates travel). Members, profiles and projects refer to this
495: paragraph for exclusions and do not decide them again (P5, P6, P1).
496: Purpose and material relation: an exclusion read as a result of the
497: excluded obligation, or a "does not apply" counted toward that obligation,
498: would report a skipped obligation as met; an unresolved premise read as an
499: exclusion would drop an obligation that may apply.

502: 
503: The step's output is a candidate plus its step-produced facts: judgments with
504: their evidence, residuals, observations, authority decisions, provenance, and
505: one stop state, a value of construction availability (§3). The actor's
506: internal path is not part of the output
507: and is not governed (FOUNDATIONS §2, work boundary; provenance
508: [Probabilistic Work Boundary](stdo://releases/v2.5.1-rc.2/standards/SPEC_METHOD.md#probabilistic-work-boundary)).

510: ### Configuration
511: 
512: A configuration allocates a task's responsibilities and information
513: exchanges to frames; activation binds an actor to each frame (§2). It
514: spans the task's work. The active frame configuration of
515: [FOUNDATIONS §2](FOUNDATIONS.md#frame-objects) is the part of it used for
516: one evaluation or decision (P0, P6).
517: 
518: Allocation. The allocation is derived from the task's operations, the
519: dependencies among them, the capabilities of the available actors and the
520: required assurance. These conditions constrain a configuration without
521: determining it. One frame or several, fixed or adaptive allocation, a
522: hierarchy, peer coordination, or an arrangement that changes as actors
523: allocate work within their contracts may each be adequate. The method
524: prescribes no role inventory, hierarchy, coordinator or actor sequence
525: (P0, P2; [RF-016](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#rf-016-implementation-neutrality)).
526: 
527: Explanation. The design that selects a configuration explains its
528: allocation, including the material resource limits it assumes, such as
529: context, attention, time and spend, each declared as §6 Resource limits
530: states, and its assumptions about failure. The
531: explanation is recorded in the existing design, frame declarations and
532: work records; no separate configuration carrier is required. It is
533: proportional (§6): for a direct entry, the intake states it, its
534: capability envelopes carrying the resource limits and its stale,
535: out-of-scope and re-entry conditions the failure assumptions (P2, P6).
536: 
537: Direct entry. A one-frame configuration is lawful. One directly
538: authorised task that fits a sufficient frame may be entered directly, as
539: Roles and delegation below states, subject to any separately required
540: assessment (P0, P2).
541: 
542: Evaluation of a configuration. A configuration is evaluated against the
543: task, its dependencies, the actors' limits, the authority it applies and
544: the required assurance. Its labels, its topology, and the presence or
545: absence of a coordinator or of familiar roles neither accept nor reject
546: it (P1, P6).
547: 
548: Distinct relations. Within a configuration, the relation structures that
549: [RF-009](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#rf-009-no-assumed-hierarchy) lists as
550: distinct stay distinct; RF-009 owns that list and the rule, and this
551: section adds no structure to it and drops none. Naming or selecting a
552: frame grants no authority
553: ([FOUNDATIONS §2 Authority kinds](FOUNDATIONS.md#authority-kinds), What
554: creates no authority): each activation binds its subject, result algebra,
555: actor capability and admitted authority through its intake (Intake above)
556: and its activation (§2).
557: 
558: Admission of a configuration. A configuration is admitted by the smallest
559: existing authority that owns the material decision the configuration
560: makes. One configuration may derive from several governing owners; each
561: keeps its own decision, and admitting the configuration creates no new
562: authority over them. An explicitly bounded proxy admits a configuration
563: only inside its existing grant (FOUNDATIONS §2 Authority kinds, Proxy
564: limit). Which authority owns which decision is a binding of the method or
565: profile that applies this rule; the executive configuration's binding is
566: [EXECUTIVE_BASELINE §16](EXECUTIVE_BASELINE.md#existing-authority-selects-the-configuration),
567: which refers here for the rule (P6, P2; provenance
568: [Existing Authority Selects The Configuration](stdo://releases/v2.5.1-rc.2/standards/STDO_REFERENCE_FRAME_BASELINE.md#existing-authority-selects-the-configuration)).
569: Purpose and material relation: a configuration allocates authority-bearing
570: responsibilities, so admitting one by an authority that does not own the
571: decision would move authority with position in the decomposition; the
572: rule holds for every configuration, so it is stated here and not only in
573: an optional profile.
574: 
575: Reconfiguration. The allocation, and every reconfiguration, preserves the
576: material dependencies, the outstanding obligations, the effects,
577: including those of uncertain completion, the admitted authority and the
578: required independence. A reconfiguration is any change of allocation
579: during the work, including actors redistributing work among themselves
580: and the changes §4 Reconfiguration selects after a result. An outstanding
581: obligation and the results that depend on it move with the work. An
582: effect of uncertain completion is resolved sufficiently for the next
583: dependent action before that action (§3 Effects, §5). Required
584: independence keeps its authorship and evidence conditions
585: ([EVALUATION §3](EVALUATION.md#3-independent-assessment)), which a fresh
586: context alone does not meet. An adaptive or self-organised arrangement is
587: exempt from none of these conditions (P4b, P5, P6).
588: 
589: Coordination across tasks. Where a configuration separates carrying out a
590: bounded change from managing delivery across successive tasks, the frame
591: that manages delivery, a coordinator, keeps the longer-term outcome. It
592: decides from a sufficient bounded view of the closed results of the task
593: frames (§7), not from their working context. When a decision needs
594: information that view lacks, the coordinator acquires it through its
595: permitted frame inputs or requests a further bounded evaluation. Missing
596: information is never assumed favourable. A task result never omits a
597: blocker material to delivery: a compact result keeps every residual and
598: stop material to delivery, and its size is no evidence that none exists
599: (P0, P4b, P6).
600: 
601: Optimality. Adequacy and optimality are separate claims. A claim that a
602: configuration is optimal is relative to stated objectives, constraints and
603: comparison scope, and rests on evidence for that comparison. Familiarity,
604: persistence or self-organisation is no such evidence. Different
605: objectives or actor models can favour different configurations (P2, P6).
606: 
607: Example. The executive configuration, which the
608: [executive baseline profile](EXECUTIVE_BASELINE.md) defines, is one
609: configuration, for task-based work in which a bounded change and delivery
610: across tasks need separate attention. It allocates construction,
611: assessment and disposition (Roles and delegation below) to three frames: a
612: Worker frame, specialised as Writer for documents and records, constructs;
613: a Reviewer frame assesses; an Executive frame keeps the longer-term
614: outcome, selects the next work and disposes within its decision grant, and
615: mutates nothing. That profile owns its frames, their grants, limits and
616: prohibitions, and its explanation; they apply where it is selected. It is
617: not the method's default. A configuration with other frames, names or
618: topology is evaluated as Evaluation of a configuration above states.
619: 
620: Purpose and material relation: finite context and attention bound each
621: responsibility (P0); dependencies between tasks require exchanges and
622: compatible results (P4a); and fallible actors with incomplete knowledge
623: require checking and recovery (P5, P6). These conditions constrain a
624: configuration without determining it, so the method states what every
625: allocation preserves and leaves the arrangement to the design. A
626: coordinator that decides from a bounded view relies on what the view
627: holds, so a blocker missing from a task result would be read as absent
628: (P4b). The material relation is whether an allocation or a reallocation
629: can drop an obligation, an effect or a required independence, move
630: authority with position in a decomposition, or hide a blocker from the
631: decision that needs it. This law is new in this corpus.

633: ### Roles and delegation
634: 
635: Roles are relations over steps that a configuration allocates
636: (Configuration above), not kinds of actor and not a fixed inventory.
637: Where a configuration's operations include construction, assessment or
638: disposition, this member names the responsibility for each: a
639: constructor owns the bounded implementation and its self-review; an
640: assessor evaluates a frozen candidate independently; a decision owner
641: disposes. A configuration may allocate other responsibilities. Where
642: independent assessment supports promotion, the assessor evaluates the
643: exact live subject, its authority paths, proof and installed state, not
644: the constructor's summary (EVALUATION §3). This preserves the
645: independent-review relation and prescribes no actor count: one actor may
646: occupy different roles across different claims when independence for the
647: assessed claim is preserved; no role name confers an operation
648: ([RF-016](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#rf-016-implementation-neutrality);
649: P6; provenance rc.2 SPEC_METHOD #construction-assessment-and-delegation).
650: 
651: The governing authority relation declares whether a bounded proxy may
652: accept and advance routine actions. Inside a step, a proxy so declared may
653: accept preservation, require local repair, reject a violating candidate
654: and advance to the next already-authorised action; what it cannot change,
655: and the re-entry such a change requires, is the proxy limit of
656: [FOUNDATIONS §2 Authority kinds](FOUNDATIONS.md#authority-kinds), which
657: this paragraph applies and does not restate. Human ceremony is not
658: required at every transition when the governing basis, the affected cone,
659: the decision envelope and the re-entry conditions are already accepted;
660: this delegation does not weaken direct human acceptance where the
661: governing product or release law requires it (P6).
662: 
663: When one capable context has sufficient existing work authority, exact
664: intake and operation grant, it may enter directly as constructor. No
665: coordinating activation is required solely to appoint that constructor.
666: The applicable configuration (Configuration above) selects
667: coordination when material contexts, authority, capability or required
668: independence need it; a role or frame name alone does not create that
669: need. Direct entry preserves
670: execution-contract admission (Admission above), the smallest lawful
671: carrier, the required assessment and the reserved decisions; it permits
672: bounded work and condition-based result recording, not self-acceptance at a
673: boundary owned elsewhere (P0, P6).
674: 
675: Recording a result without review. Where the applicable law requires no
676: independent assessment and reserves no acceptance, the constructor that
677: holds the grant may record the satisfied bounded result, with the conditions
678: it found satisfied and their evidence, in the existing durable result
679: surface (Admission above), without adding a carrier, coordinating
680: appointment, review or recreated design view solely to mark completion. The
681: recording waives no condition that actually applies, states only
682: conditions that were evaluated, as an actor cannot report an evaluation it
683: did not perform
684: ([FOUNDATIONS §4 Results](FOUNDATIONS.md#results), Not performed), and is
685: no disposition at a boundary owned elsewhere
686: (§3 Disposition). Where an assessment or a reserved acceptance does apply,
687: it stays required, and an insufficiency found during the work withholds
688: the dependent effect and returns the question to its owner. Carriers and
689: profiles refer to this paragraph and add only their own carrier binding
690: (P0, P2, P6; provenance rc.2 SPEC_METHOD
691: #construction-assessment-and-delegation). Purpose and material relation:
692: requiring a review or a ceremony that no law requires adds reasoning cost
693: without removing ambiguity (§6), while recording a satisfied result where
694: a review or acceptance is required would read construction as acceptance.
695: 
696: One bounded operation grant may cover its declared construction, status and
697: checkpoint sequence. Actual role transitions are recorded in the existing
698: work carrier; a status change alone requires neither a new grant nor
699: rehashing an unchanged subject. A material change to the operation, the
700: subject, the territory, the authority or the validity conditions requires
701: the affected binding to be resolved before effect. Where the selected
702: configuration restricts its coordinating role from mutation, as the
703: executive configuration (Configuration above) restricts its Executive,
704: the restriction that the coordinating role mutates nothing remains
705: applicable; an operation grant covering the sequence does not lift it
706: (P6, P3).

708: ## 2. Context construction under an envelope
709: 
710: Given a seed set Z (the subject, outcome and affected relations named in the
711: intake), a relation family R (the dependency relations whose change can
712: alter the result, basis validity, evidence sufficiency or authority), and
713: the actor's envelope B:
714: 
715: 1. Compute the material closure of Z under R (AC-010,
716:    [AXIOMATIC_CALCULUS §4](AXIOMATIC_CALCULUS.md#ac-010-material-closure);
717:    provenance rc.2 AXIOMATIC_CALCULUS #ac-010-material-closure).
718:    If Z, R, the required-edge semantics, the basis or the scope is not
719:    exact, or the closure is not finite and unique on the exact basis,
720:    activation returns `closure_unresolved` (§3, Admission and activation)
721:    and no step proceeds; it does not guess.
722: 2. Project the closure to the selected context, recording its boundary: the
723:    excluded, external, unresolved, translated and invalidation-sensitive
724:    seams (AC-011,
725:    [AXIOMATIC_CALCULUS §4](AXIOMATIC_CALCULUS.md#ac-011-projection);
726:    provenance rc.2 AXIOMATIC_CALCULUS #ac-011-projection).
727:    If the projection returns a gap or refusal, activation returns
728:    `closure_unresolved` and no step proceeds.
729:    Material not in the closure stays out; adjacent material does not enter
730:    because it exists
731:    ([RF-004](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#rf-004-irrelevance-exclusion)).
732: 3. Test fit against B. This step owns the size measurement and the fit
733:    test for the context dimension. Where B declares a context capacity,
734:    the size of the selected context is measured before the actor reads it,
735:    in the unit the capacity is declared in (bytes or tokens) and over the
736:    boundary that the selected execution binding states for it (§6), and is
737:    compared with that capacity. The measurement, the capacity and the
738:    outcome of the comparison are recorded before the read. A fit holds
739:    only for the measured boundary: context outside it is recorded as not
740:    measured, and no fit claim covers it. The fit claim for a measured
741:    boundary and the step's fit judgment are distinct: the judgment is
742:    established from the recorded complete active inputs, or from a
743:    supported upper bound that includes any host-supplied context; where
744:    neither exists it is `indeterminate`, unless a counterexample, such as
745:    a measured excess, establishes `falsified`. A correct task result keeps
746:    its own bounded standing and does not satisfy a missing fit judgment
747:    (P0, P5; new law carried at iteration 4 from the common construction
748:    contract's Limits). The measurement applies wherever B declares a
749:    context capacity, whatever question the step governs, because without
750:    it a fit claim against that capacity is an assertion, and a context
751:    that exceeds it would be cut without record
752:    ([RF-014](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#rf-014-capability-fit)). It applies only
753:    there, because a resource control applies only through a selected frame
754:    and its owning scope
755:    ([REFERENCE_FRAME §4](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#frame-set-selection)), and a
756:    declared capacity is such a control: the measurement tests that
757:    capacity, is no budget, and is not a universal measurement gate (P0,
758:    P2). Where B declares no context capacity, no measurement is
759:    required, and the other conditions of
760:    [RF-002](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#rf-002-finite-attention) still apply. The size
761:    shows fit and nothing else: whether the actor attends adequately to a
762:    context that fits is decided by actual-use evidence under
763:    [EVALUATION §7](EVALUATION.md#frame-qualification). The envelope's
764:    other dimensions are fitted as RF-014 states; time, spend and other
765:    resource limits follow [§6 Resource limits](#resource-limits), which
766:    this step refines for the context dimension.
767:    If the context fits, activate. If it does not, one or more of these, in
768:    combination: refine the outer contract into steps whose contexts fit
769:    (§4); sequence activations; apply a lawful compression that preserves the material
770:    distinctions and failure detections the evaluation needs
771:    ([RF-017](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#rf-017-proportional-compression));
772:    select another actor whose envelope fits; or return `out_of_frame`
773:    ([§3 Admission and activation](#admission-and-activation)). Silent
774:    omission to fit is never one of them
775:    ([RF-014](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#rf-014-capability-fit)).
776:    None of these options silently reduces the required predicate: the
777:    obligations the step preserves stay bound
778:    ([§1 Transitions](#transitions)), a narrowing of them is a destination
779:    change under its owning authority (§1, Destination change), and what
780:    the selected context leaves undecided stays an explicit unknown.
781:    Within its grant a step selects refinement, sequencing and compression
782:    itself; selecting another actor or enlarging the envelope re-enters the
783:    owner of the grant or envelope, who may delegate that choice in the grant
784:    (RF-014 vests the selection in human authority; a grant is that authority
785:    exercised in advance). Whether a compression preserved what the
786:    evaluation needs is a claim with the standing of any other (EVALUATION
787:    §1): the compressing step records the compression and its preservation
788:    argument, and the claim is assessed independently only where the
789:    promotion it serves needs independent assessment (EVALUATION §3).
790: 4. Acquire declared inputs by their exact identity. A declared input absent
791:    from the actor's materialised view is neither licence to read elsewhere
792:    nor evidence that it is absent.
793: 
794: Acquisition. Acquisition is the read-only observation of the material a
795: step needs (its frame, its subject and its declared inputs) within the
796: inputs its intake permits it to acquire (§1 Intake). It is not an admission and not a further
797: actor stage; effectful work still needs its admitted contract (§1
798: Admission), and acquiring material grants no role, operation or decision
799: ([FOUNDATIONS §2 Authority kinds](FOUNDATIONS.md#authority-kinds), What
800: creates no authority). A derived surface is material derived from owning
801: sources by copying, projection or compression, such as a projected frame
802: for a task; it names its sources and owns no determination. Where a
803: current interpretation of the subject in `a_c` exists, the constraint
804: surface it yields for the task is such a derived surface, under the
805: conditions of
806: [AXIOMATIC_CALCULUS §9 Constraint surfaces](AXIOMATIC_CALCULUS.md#constraint-surfaces).
807: Where a derived surface whose sources
808: are current serves the task, the actor acquires it. For another task, or
809: where the derived surface is stale ([Staleness of derived surfaces](#staleness-of-derived-surfaces)),
810: missing or insufficient for a named unresolved question, the actor acquires the deciding owner sections
811: directly: the owning sections that decide the task's questions, which are
812: declared inputs of the selected basis. Only material in the exact selected
813: basis is acquired: where that basis has no derived surface for the task,
814: its exact owner sections are used, and a candidate source, a version-line
815: selector or another basis is no substitute for it
816: ([FOUNDATIONS §2 Product lineage objects](FOUNDATIONS.md#product-lineage-objects)).
817: The actor reads the selected material and the actual subject, and follows
818: the material source and coupled-obligation references the task needs
819: (step 2; [RF-003](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#rf-003-material-sufficiency));
820: presence of the corpus is no reason to read it (step 2;
821: [RF-004](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#rf-004-irrelevance-exclusion)). No frame,
822: derived surface or acquisition carries a universal sufficiency claim:
823: sufficiency is decided for the named question against the task's
824: material relations, never for the corpus or the frame as a whole
825: ([RF-003](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#rf-003-material-sufficiency)). Before any dependent action, a missing binding is returned with
826: its outcome of [§3 Admission and activation](#admission-and-activation),
827: naming what is missing: where no available frame declares what the
828: evaluation needs, `out_of_frame`; where the basis the step requires is
829: absent, or cannot be verified as the exact basis named, the basis cannot
830: support the step, and the outcome is `admission_refused(basis)` at
831: admission or `activation_refusal(basis)` at activation. Coupled material that does not fit is handled under
832: step 3 (P0, P6, P3).
833: 
834: Activation. The actor or the assigning authority selects one frame or one
835: declared active frame configuration capable of the evaluation. Activation
836: binds each reusable frame declaration to the exact subject and basis and
837: loads the declared material relations and evidence sources, never an
838: undifferentiated workspace dump; what the actor receives is the packet of
839: REFERENCE_FRAME §11. When no available frame and actor pairing can lawfully
840: perform the evaluation, activation returns `out_of_frame` where no such
841: pairing declares a material relation or capability the evaluation needs,
842: and otherwise the `activation_refusal` whose cause holds
843: ([REFERENCE_FRAME §5](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#5-method-functions)); both are
844: outcomes of §3 Admission and activation. Work does not proceed through
845: implied context (P0, P5; rc.2 RF #select-and-activate).
846: 
847: Evaluation. The evaluator applies the activated frame's declared
848: invariants, evidence rules and result algebra. It distinguishes semantic
849: law, observed evidence, inference and decision, and records every
850: assumption whose failure could change the result (P6; rc.2 RF #evaluate).
851: 
852: Standalone scope. A derived surface or projected frame decides standalone
853: only the questions that its own source members own. It refuses to close a
854: question owned by another member, or one that joins the law of several
855: owners; the evaluation of that question returns `out_of_frame`
856: ([FOUNDATIONS §4 Results](FOUNDATIONS.md#results)) naming the owning
857: members, and
858: the question is decided from those members at their current digests or
859: from a derived surface whose declared sources include every owner of it
860: (P6, P1; provenance rc.2 authority_compressions/stdo_compressed.md,
861: spec_method.compressed.md and design_module_method.compressed.md, a claim
862: no rc.2 body states).
863: 
864: Prompt construction. A prompt is rendered contract over typed authority;
865: it is not source law. Prompt text must project the current authority
866: references, obligations, contracts, boundaries, proof expectations and
867: fallback conditions the step needs. A prompt body must not carry
868: historical workaround prose unless the current requirement, design,
869: runtime or test still needs that constraint. Construction starts from the
870: highest live authority surface the question needs. If the prompt needs an
871: upstream ambiguity resolved, it names the unresolved authority gap before
872: fallback context is read. The prompt projects the material that
873: Acquisition above selects, including the raw owning members where
874: Acquisition falls back to them (P6, P0, P5; provenance rc.2
875: authority_compressions/stdo_compressed.md and spec_method.compressed.md, a

1123: 
1124: An effectful step's intended external effect has one of three values,
1125: mapped for the predicate "the intended effect occurred", that is, the
1126: declared outcome of the effect is attained. The value turns on what the
1127: admitted evidence establishes about that outcome (P5 with the open-world
1128: premise, P6):
1129: 
1130: | Value | Core value | Ground |
1131: |---|---|---|
1132: | `completed` | satisfied | evidence about the subject establishes that the intended effect occurred in full and that the effect has ended |
1133: | `not_effected` | falsified | evidence about the subject establishes that the declared outcome is not attained and that no later completion of the effect can attain it, for example where no effect began, the effect ended short, an interval that the declared outcome bounds has closed with part of it not done, or the part already done has itself made the declared outcome unattainable, as when it destroyed what the outcome needed; any part that did occur is named with the value |
1134: | `completion_unknown` | indeterminate | neither of the other values is established: the evidence cannot decide whether the intended effect has occurred or will occur, as for a request whose response was lost with no counterexample observed; or the evidence shows the intended effect in full while the effect may still be acting, and the outcome stays undecided until what the effect leaves on the subject is settled; its residual names the observation that would decide |
1135: 
1136: `not_effected` requires evidence about the subject. A lost response, an
1137: absent record, or an actor's context that holds no trace of the effect is
1138: not that evidence, and the value stays `completion_unknown`.
1139: `completion_unknown` never maps to `completed` or to `not_effected`.
1140: 
1141: Whether the effect has ended is a second question beside the predicate
1142: above, and it enters the value only as a conjunct of `completed`. An
1143: effect has ended where evidence about the subject establishes
1144: that it no longer acts on the subject or that it never began. Where an
1145: effect began and the evidence does not establish its end, the end is not
1146: established, and that is recorded beside the value as a residual naming
1147: the observation that would establish it. The end never decides a
1148: `not_effected` value and is never folded into one: when an interval that
1149: the declared outcome bounds has closed with part not done, the value is
1150: `not_effected` while the effect may still be acting. The residual does
1151: not weaken the counterexample, and the counterexample does not resolve
1152: the residual. `completed` alone requires the end established, so a
1153: counterexample stands without proof that the effect has ended, while
1154: success is never claimed for an effect that may still be acting
1155: ([FOUNDATIONS §4 Results](FOUNDATIONS.md#results), Standing).
1156: 
1157: An effect of uncertain completion is one whose value is
1158: `completion_unknown`, or whose value is `not_effected` with its end not
1159: established. §5 reconciles it, sufficiently for the next dependent
1160: action, before any repetition or dependent action. A `not_effected`
1161: value licenses neither treating the effect as absent nor repeating it
1162: while its end is not established.
1163:

1165: external effect may have occurred is repeated or relied on; §4 names a

1169: counterexample already established for the declared outcome, and an

1356: 
1357: The evidence above is not a documentation deliverable. It is the durable

1359: action generally does not hold the reasoning that produced the governing
1360: frames. Dialogue, commentary, prior-agent memory and implementation

1363: reconstruction rule is [ACTIVATION §1 Intake](ACTIVATION.md#intake); this
1364: clause states what design evidence supplies to it.
1365: 
1366: Given an accepted material-frame basis, as determined by the material
1367: semantic boundary definition (§5), and a proposed bounded action, the
1368: selected method basis together with the accepted design is sufficient to
1369: recover every governing relation in the action's causally closed affected
1370: set, and to decide whether the action preserves, changes, duplicates or
1371: violates each of those relations. The two inputs are distinct and neither
1372: substitutes for the other: the selected method describes which relations
1373: are required and how to reason about them; the accepted design supplies
1374: the concrete invariant values. A design that restates method decision law
1375: to satisfy this rule reproduces the local-method duplication this law
1376: exists to remove (P1, P6).
1377: 
1378: | Decision | The action, against a governing relation | Core value |
1379: |---|---|---|
1380: | preserves | leaves the relation intact and operates inside it | `satisfied` |
1381: | changes | alters the relation itself, and therefore re-enters at that relation before acceptance | `invalid_basis` |
1382: | duplicates | establishes an equivalent path that is explicitly disposed as a constituent, replica, replacement or deliberately independent realisation | `satisfied` |
1383: | violates | contradicts the relation, or establishes an equivalent path that is left undisposed | `falsified` |
1384: 
1385: The boundary between changes and violates is the locus of acceptance, not
1386: the size of the delta: an otherwise lawful change accepted inside the
1387: acting frame rather than at the relation it changes is a violation. The
1388: boundary between duplicates and violates is disposition, not equivalence:
1389: an equivalent path is lawful when disposed and a defect when not (P4b,
1390: P6). Sufficiency is bound to a declared action and its affected set; it is
1391: not a claim over an unbounded space of possible future changes, and the
1392: performance of any particular reviewer is not evidence either way (P0).
1393: 
1394: The four decisions are a refinement of the core result algebra
1395: ([FOUNDATIONS §4 Results](FOUNDATIONS.md#results)). They answer what the
1396: action does to one governing relation, and each maps to exactly one core
1397: value for the predicate "the action is admissible in the acting frame on
1398: the current governing basis"; the value travels with that predicate and
1399: with the decision, whose meaning stays here (P5, P6). `preserves` and a
1400: disposed `duplicates` leave the relation as accepted. For `changes`, the
1401: relation itself must change at its own locus before the action can be
1402: accepted, so the current governing basis cannot support accepting it in
1403: the acting frame, as for the disposition `re_enter` of
1404: [ACTIVATION §3](ACTIVATION.md#disposition). For `violates`, the
1405: contradiction or the undisposed equivalent path is the counterexample. An
1406: evaluation that reaches none of the four decisions returns the core value

1008: ### Conjunction of results
1009: 
1010: Conjunction combines results into one result for a composite predicate: a
1011: claim made of component claims, a decision over several evaluations, or
1012: the outer contract of composed steps. It is defined here once, over the
1013: core. A member or profile that combines results applies this rule and
1014: declares only the choices it leaves open (P4a, P4b, P5, P6). Purpose and
1015: material relation: failure, undecidability, non-performance and uncertain
1016: effects stay distinct after combination, so a composite can neither report
1017: success nor hide which kind of gap it has while one of them remains in
1018: what it requires.
1019: 
1020: A conjunction takes three inputs and keeps them apart:
1021: 
1022: 1. the closed results returned, each a core value with its predicate and
1023:    domain outcome;
1024: 2. the planned assessments that have no closed result applicable to the
1025:    composite: evaluations the composite requires that were not performed,
1026:    or whose result is absent, stale, bound to another subject or basis, or
1027:    incapable of establishing its claim;
1028: 3. the required coverage that no planned assessment addresses: for a
1029:    required obligation set R and the coverage C that the planned
1030:    assessments admit, the uncovered coverage R minus C.
1031: 
1032: Gaps. A conjunction with any item in the second or third input is never
1033: `satisfied`. Its record names which kind of gap it has, not performed,
1034: uncovered or both, and each item. An uncovered obligation is never
1035: reported as satisfied or as an omitted planned assessment; a planned
1036: assessment without a closed result is never reported as uncovered or as an
1037: `indeterminate` component.
1038: 
1039: Stale results. A held result is stale for a composite when the basis it
1040: binds is not the basis the composite requires of that component, because
1041: a later state has superseded or advanced it or because it binds another
1042: subject or basis (§2, Basis). Two questions stay apart, so that one
1043: composite has one reported value (P3, P5, P6):
1044: 
1045: - an evaluation performed on a subject or basis that is stale, advanced,
1046:   ambiguous or mismatched against the one it must bind returns
1047:   `invalid_basis` for its own predicate, or is reactivated on the current
1048:   basis; [RF-005](REFERENCE_FRAME.md#rf-005-exact-basis-and-coordinates)
1049:   states this law over frames, and a conjunction that receives that
1050:   `invalid_basis` holds it as a returned result;
1051: - a component whose only held result is stale has no closed result
1052:   applicable to the composite: on the basis the composite requires, its
1053:   evaluation was not performed. It is a planned assessment without a
1054:   closed result, in the second input, and a gap. Its stale value is
1055:   neither counted as a returned result nor relabelled `invalid_basis` for
1056:   the composite, and it stays in the record with its own basis;
1057: - the declared basis relations disagree (Reported value below) only among
1058:   returned results, each bound to the basis the composite requires of it,
1059:   whose bases the declared rule requires to agree or to join in a coherent
1060:   composite basis, and which do not.
1061: 
1062: A stale `satisfied` never counts toward the composite. Purpose and
1063: material relation: answering one stale component as a gap in one place and
1064: as `invalid_basis` in another would give one composite two reported
1065: values; keeping the evaluation's own value apart from the composite's gap
1066: keeps the reported value unique and the stale result visible.
1067: 
1068: Survival. Every known failure (a `falsified` result with its
1069: counterexample), every planned assessment without a closed result and
1070: every uncovered obligation is carried with the composite and survives every
1071: join: combining conjunctions takes the union of known failures and of
1072: planned assessments without a closed result, and recomputes uncovered
1073: coverage over the combined obligations. A known failure and a gap of
1074: either kind survive every join. Common agreement among contributors
1075: cannot discard a constraint or a material counterexample that only one
1076: contributor found. A missing, wrong-subject, conflicted or indeterminate
1077: mandatory result stays unresolved under the declared rule, and aggregate
1078: satisfied status cannot replace it (P4b, P5).
1079: 
1080: Predicates travel. Each component keeps its predicate and domain outcome
1081: through every join, so an uncertain effect stays distinct from a failure
1082: and from an undecided claim, and a `satisfied` for one predicate never
1083: counts toward another: a step's `satisfied` for "a candidate is available"
1084: never counts toward the candidate's fitness or acceptance.
1085: 
1086: Reported value. The conjunction is `satisfied` only when every mandatory
1087: result is `satisfied` with valid applicable evidence, the declared basis
1088: relations agree, and the second and third inputs are empty. A composite
1089: with a material counterexample to a mandatory constraint or an uncovered
1090: required obligation cannot project success. Where a mandatory result is
1091: not `satisfied`, the conjunction
1092: reports a non-satisfied value chosen by its declared rule from those
1093: returned; no universal order among the non-satisfied values exists, and
1094: the member or profile that applies the conjunction declares its own in
1095: its declared rule. Where every mandatory returned result is `satisfied`
1096: and the declared basis relations disagree, the conjunction reports
1097: `invalid_basis`, because no coherent composite basis supports the
1098: composite predicate (§2, Basis); any gaps stay recorded by kind. Where
1099: every mandatory returned result is `satisfied`, the declared basis
1100: relations agree and a gap remains, the conjunction reports
1101: `indeterminate`, because the results it holds cannot decide the composite
1102: predicate; that value is the conjunction's own, and the gaps stay
1103: recorded by kind. A `falsified` result that is not mandatory stays in the
1104: conjunction's record with its predicate and is never discarded. Where
1105: every mandatory result is `satisfied`, the declared basis relations agree
1106: and no gap remains, a material counterexample to a mandatory constraint
1107: falsifies the conjunction whichever contributor found it, and without one
1108: the conjunction's value is the one its declared rule gives over the
1109: mandatory results.
1110: 
1111: The declared rule. A conjunction declares which results are mandatory; how
1112: conflict and indeterminacy are handled; which basis relations must agree,
1113: results on different bases combining only under a declared coherent
1114: composite basis (§2, Basis); which decision authority owns the
1115: conjunction; how results that are not mandatory count; and whether any
1116: result may veto or defer the decision.
1117: Conjunction does not transfer the semantic authority of a contributing
1118: evaluation (P6, P4a).
1119: 
1120: Composition of results. The result of composed steps for the outer
1121: predicate is the conjunction of the steps' results, with the outer
1122: contract's obligations as the required coverage. That each step returned
1123: `satisfied` supports the outer predicate only where the refinement's
1124: premises hold ([ACTIVATION §4](ACTIVATION.md#4-totality-and-refinement),
1125: Refinement).

1231: 
1232: For a selected source obligation r, let `Q_r = interpret(r, Γ, Ω)` be its
1233: required predicate over the declared domain Ω of cases. The interpretation
1234: retains the obligation's scope, conditions, exceptions and permitted
1235: alternatives. A source identifier or digest authenticates the selected
1236: text; it does not prove the interpretation faithful (P1, P3).
1237: 
1238: An assertion targets a predicate `q_t` and states whether that predicate
1239: is the full obligation or a narrower claim. The directions differ:
1240: 
1241: ```text
1242: Q_r implies q_t                   : q_t is a necessary condition of Q_r.
1243: q_t implies Q_r                   : establishing q_t is sufficient for Q_r.
1244: (q_1 and ... and q_n) implies Q_r : the stated conjunction is sufficient.
1245: ```
1246: 
1247: These implications are conditional on Γ and Ω. Passing a check of a
1248: necessary condition does not establish the full obligation. A conjunction
1249: requires its own coverage and compatibility argument; a collection of
1250: passing tests does not supply it by its count (P1, P6, P5).
1251: 
1252: Let `π: Ω → O` describe the observations available to a checker and
1253: `T ⊆ O × R` its admitted evaluation relation, where R is the core result
1254: algebra of Results above. A deterministic checker is a functional special
1255: case. A human or probabilistic checker can admit different conclusions on
1256: the same observations; a universal soundness claim covers all admitted
1257: conclusions, not a favourable selected run. `indeterminate` is the core
1258: judgment whose admissible evidence cannot decide the predicate (Results).
1259: It is distinct from an evaluation that was not performed, and from work
1260: or coverage that remains unresolved, which is a state of the work
1261: ([ACTIVATION §3](ACTIVATION.md#3-step-results-and-disposition),
1262: [§5](ACTIVATION.md#5-work-state-and-restart)) and not a judgment value.
1263: Writing `sat` and `fal` for the satisfied and falsified judgments about
1264: `q_t`, not new execution states:
1265: 
1266: ```text
1267: For every s in Ω:
1268:   (π(s), sat) in T  implies  q_t(s).
1269:   (π(s), fal) in T  implies  not q_t(s).
1270: ```
1271: 
1272: The candidate identifies what supports each claimed direction and where
1273: it is unresolved. A relation admitting both a sound satisfied judgment
1274: and an indeterminate judgment need not always decide the case. A
1275: restricted deterministic model retains its scope, and an actual checker
1276: requires its own conformance account. An observed test pass remains an
1277: observation while the oracle's fidelity is unestablished; it does not
1278: become established satisfaction of the source obligation, and a reported
1279: failure is treated the same way. The observation binding itself needs
1280: support: an idealised π does not establish that the instrument, actor or

1282: uses its declared distribution over outcomes under Quantified assurance
1283: below; the relation alone supplies no probabilities (P6, P5, P1).

1287: 
1288: ```text
1289: For all s, s' in Ω:
1290:   π(s) = π(s')  implies  q_t(s) = q_t(s').
1291: ```

1303:

1306: derived from the requirement check its applicable conditions in the
1307: realisation; assertions derived from the refinement check its more
1308: detailed obligations there. Acceptance tests and build-level tests can
1309: instantiate these paths
1310: ([ASSURANCE §14](ASSURANCE.md#14-design-to-module-evidence-route)); the
1311: roles identify the selected sources and subject in this example and
1312: prescribe no stage names or test classes (P1, P6).
1313: 
1314: An expected result or acceptance predicate has assertion provenance: the
1315: owning obligation, its interpretation, assumptions, derivation and shared
1316: dependencies, which give it meaning. An execution has execution
1317: provenance: the actual checker, exact subject, applicable environment,
1318: inputs, observations and result. Both are needed for the claims they
1319: support; execution records alone cannot establish that the expected
1320: answer follows from the requirement. Authentication of a returned
1321: assessment, which binds a result to its producer, subject, basis and
1322: evidence ([EVALUATION §3](EVALUATION.md#3-independent-assessment)), is a
1323: third thing and establishes neither provenance (P6, P3).

1326: local property. Repeating the implementation's decision supplies no
1327: independent justification of its fidelity to an upstream obligation. A
1328: shorter assertion path can be easier to inspect and execute than
1329: construction; that is a supported design claim for the selected path, not
1330: a guarantee of correctness, coverage or independence. Valid distinct
1331: implementations can produce different outputs within the same acceptable
1332: relation; equality of outputs is not always the oracle (P1, P6).

1335: assertions within their stated scope. Agreement is compatible with both
1336: being correct and with a shared error; disagreement requires diagnosis

1339: shared specifications, interpretations, tools, fixtures, observations,
1340: copied material and other material failure causes. Different actors,

# Axiomatic Calculus Implementations

## Product boundary

This source project constructs implementations of the **axiomatic calculus**,
`a_c`, identified by `urn:stdo:concept:axiomatic-calculus:a-c`. Its bounded context
is `urn:a-c-implementations:bounded-context:realization`.

The exact selected published STDO `AXIOMATIC_CALCULUS.md` owns calculus meaning.
The shared requirements here own implementation obligations. A tenant's design
owns its computational calculus, representation, operations, concrete stack and
demonstrated fragment. Neither an implementation nor its successful use amends
the calculus or accepts a subject interpretation.

## Capability

A realization consumes an explicitly authored symbolic model and its exact
signature and basis. It checks a declared structural fragment, preserves the
model through its representation, and supports source-reenterable inspection
and bounded acquisition. It keeps constraints, latitude, residuals, traversals,
transformations and judgments distinguishable from ordinary objects and edges.

The initial Graphify realization carries all eight fundamental record families
and their exact content. Its mechanical checks and acquisition operations have
an explicitly bounded contract. It does not execute arbitrary predicates,
transform models, select applicable frames, infer logical truth or supply
semantic acceptance. Unsupported operations refuse rather than silently pass.

A tenant may also facilitate creation of that model from an exact source
corpus through a declared F_P authoring workflow. Graphify extraction and
discovery may supply authoring context inside this workflow; an existing
native interpretation is not a prerequisite. The workflow retains exact source
grounding, authoring provenance, coverage dispositions and unresolved meaning.
Authoring, mechanical validation, carrier generation and external semantic
assessment remain separate claims. A generated model starts as a proposal.

An unaccepted model may have a clearly identified **proposal view**. That view
is not `a_c.X.C` admission. An encoding represented as the calculus's accepted-
interpretation carrier relation requires a separate semantic judgment over the
exact unchanged model. Tooling checks the supplied coordinates; decision
authority and semantic fitness remain external judgments. Carrier admission is
a separate judgment over unchanged carrier bytes; creating bytes or passing a
partial check does not itself issue that judgment. Its declared assessment may
be mechanical, as in `F_D[v_carrier_admission]`.
