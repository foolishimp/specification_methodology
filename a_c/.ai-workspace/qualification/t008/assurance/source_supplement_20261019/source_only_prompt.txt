191:

1782: ## 11. Build-tenant binding
1783: 
1784: This section owns the relation between a product's WHAT and the build
1785: tenants that realise it: what a tenant binds, what stays fixed while it
1786: chooses, and what its evidence shows. It applies to every product built
1787: with this method (P2, P1, P6).
1788: 
1789: Terms used in this section:
1790: 
1791: - A build tenant is one independent, project-owned HOW realisation of a
1792:   product's WHAT. The build-tenancy context of
1793:   [PRODUCT_DEFINITION §8](PRODUCT_DEFINITION.md#8-what-how-and-work-carriers)
1794:   names it, and the product definition's tenant registry locates each one.
1795: - The requirements calculus of a product is the set of obligations its
1796:   WHAT states, the outcomes it permits, and its rules for refining and
1797:   composing the work that realises it.
1798: - A computational calculus is a declared set of executable operations,
1799:   with the laws by which they compose, the transformations they permit,
1800:   and their effects and resource behaviour, as far as these are material
1801:   to the obligations.
1802: - An execution environment is the concrete means that performs those
1803:   operations: a technology stack; a human process, made of people, roles,
1804:   documents, communication channels and decision procedures; or a
1805:   combination of the two.
1806: - A tenant's build-tenant binding has two parts: its calculus binding,
1807:   from the requirements calculus to its computational calculus, and its
1808:   execution binding, from that computational calculus to its execution
1809:   environment. A tenant's registry entry locates the tenant; it is not the
1810:   binding.
1811: - A generic procedure is a method procedure whose scope is independent of
1812:   the selected execution environment. It and its projections preserve the
1813:   bindings Rule 2 permits: code, an event stream or reconstruction from a
1814:   complete history is required only where the governing obligations or
1815:   the selected execution binding require it, and an adequate human
1816:   process using current records and direct observation is a permitted
1817:   execution binding of a generic procedure that requires none of them
1818:   (P1, P6; law carried from the common binding requirement).
1819: - Author identity and actor identity are as
1820:   [EVALUATION §3](EVALUATION.md#3-independent-assessment) defines them:
1821:   authorship is one condition of an assessment's independence there, and
1822:   an actor's identity is not an authority.
1823:   Operation kind is what an operation does, such as construct, assess,
1824:   relay, record, measure or decide, together with the authority that kind
1825:   of operation requires.

1827: Rules:
1828: 
1829: 1. Realisations of one WHAT. One WHAT is realised by one or more
1830:    independent build tenants, whatever the physical layout. The WHAT is
1831:    constitutional and each tenant's realisation is HOW; a tenant-local
1832:    realisation stays derivative until its governing truth is ratified in
1833:    specification (§2). Each tenant binds the product's requirements
1834:    calculus to a declared computational calculus and binds that calculus
1835:    to a concrete execution environment. Both bindings are that tenant's
1836:    design and realisation: they are declared in its existing design and
1837:    realisation surfaces, chosen under the Design rule (§2) and recorded as
1838:    design decisions (§10), and the method requires no further carrier. The
1839:    tenant owns its binding, its realisation and their evidence; the WHAT
1840:    keeps its meaning (P2, P6).
1841: 2. Selection. The execution environment may be a technology stack, a
1842:    human process or a combination. Its selection stays within the
1843:    requirements calculus, including every human decision that calculus
1844:    requires: no choice of environment removes a required human decision or

534: ## 8. Combinatorial coverage and testing value
535: 
536: Testing value is indexed first by the material combinations a frame can
537: discriminate for its exact claim, not by test, assertion, file, line,
538: branch, mock, snapshot or runner count (P2, P6; provenance
539: #combinatorial-coverage-and-testing-value-index). For one claim, the
540: testing-value index considers at least:
541: 
542: 1. congruence between the claim and the evaluated subject;
543: 2. the number and materiality of independent claim dimensions;
544: 3. the interaction strength actually covered among those dimensions;
545: 4. the exactness of the authoritative path;
546: 5. oracle independence and precision;
547: 6. sensitivity to realistic faults, rival paths and historical failures;
548: 7. observability, reconstruction and reproducibility of the result; and
549: 8. explicit residual combinations and invalidation conditions.
550: 
551: No universal scalar is required. A project may define one only through
552: Product, requirement or accepted-design authority, and keeps the
553: underlying coverage coordinates rather than flattening them into a score
554: (P1, P6). A project-defined scalar is a quantified claim under
555: [FOUNDATIONS §4 Quantified assurance](FOUNDATIONS.md#quantified-assurance)
556: and keeps its model and limits explicit (P6).
557: 
558: At unit altitude the relevant dimensions are module-bounded. Unit-testing
559: value normally rises with the internal combinatorial complexity of an
560: algebra, calculation, state machine, transition relation or other
561: module-owned closed domain, because local tests can cheaply discriminate
562: many meaningful combinations against a precise oracle (P2).
563: 
564: At integration, end-to-end and user-acceptance altitude, testing value can
565: be multiplicative rather than additive: each material interface composes
566: preconditions, postconditions, authority, state, configuration, failure and
567: recovery with every other traversed boundary. The value comes from the
568: interactions actually discriminated, not from touching many interfaces on
569: one sunny path (P4a, P2). Material Product factors commonly include:
570: 
571: - user role and capability;
572: - supported operation and Product composition;
573: - selected definition, owner, implementation and dependency;
574: - lifecycle and persisted state;
575: - configuration and version combination;
576: - process, continuation, retry, re-entry and recovery mode;
577: - success, refusal, malformed result, partial failure and competing-path
578:   case;
579: - fresh-process, replay, reconstruction and migration condition; and
580: - user-visible outcome and projection.
581: 
582: The activated frame declares the finite interaction population and why its
583: covering set is adequate. Pairwise or other covering-array reduction is
584: lawful only where the basis explains why the omitted higher-order
585: interactions cannot change the claim, or keeps them as residuals.
586: High-consequence authority, persistence, concurrency, recovery, migration
587: and historically failure-prone relations normally need explicit
588: higher-strength combinations (P5, P2; REFERENCE_FRAME §8, interaction and
589: failure coverage).
590: 
591: Source, line, branch and function coverage may diagnose whether code was
592: visited. They do not establish semantic combination coverage,
593: authoritative path selection, user-outcome coverage or the absence of a
594: rival path (P6, P1).

716: 
717: A testing counterexample is returned to its earliest supported causal
718: frontier on the declared path. The frontier holds the causally minimal
719: violated owner relations or seam coordinates the evidence establishes;
720: earliest means minimal under the declared causal relation, not first in log
721: or clock order (P5, P6; provenance #counterexample-localization).
722: 
723: The localisation result represents exactly one of (P5, P1):
724: 
725: - one unique violated locus;
726: - a jointly established set of incomparable earliest violated loci whose
727:   combined violation is necessary to the supported causal explanation;
728: - unresolved alternative frontiers, when the evidence supports several
729:   possible causal explanations but cannot establish which occurred; or
730: - `indeterminate`, when the evidence cannot support a bounded frontier.
731: 
732: The four forms are a refinement of the core under the rule of
733: [FOUNDATIONS §4 Results](FOUNDATIONS.md#results), for the predicate "the
734: evidence establishes the counterexample's earliest violated loci on the
735: declared path". One unique violated locus and a jointly established set
736: map to `satisfied`. Unresolved alternative frontiers map to
737: `indeterminate`, naming the alternatives that further evidence must
738: decide between. The fourth form is the core value `indeterminate` itself,
739: naming the evidence a bounded frontier needs. The form travels with the
740: value as the domain outcome, so a joint set stays distinct from a unique
741: locus and unresolved alternatives stay distinct from an unbounded
742: frontier. Purpose and material relation: a localisation combines with
743: other results in the one core, and an unresolved causal explanation is
744: never read as an established one (P5, P1, P6).
745: 
746: Localisation records the subject, basis, frontier form and members, causal
747: evidence, expected relations, the observed counterexample and the
748: downstream claims it invalidates. It does not collapse a jointly
749: established set into one arbitrary owner, and it does not report
750: unresolved alternatives as a joint cause (P5, P4b).
751:

753: compatibility repair, another controller or a reinterpretation of unchanged
754: upstream meaning. A resulting scope or design change follows the existing

1308: obligations (P2, P6).
