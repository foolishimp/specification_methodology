## 4. Design adequacy criteria common to all operations

A candidate design should satisfy each of the following.

1. **Decision-completeness for the governed boundary B** (DESIGN_MODULE §5). M(B) covers:
   - entities and identities;
   - authorities and authority transitions;
   - functions and composition laws;
   - lifecycle;
   - public contracts;
   - domains and admission conditions;
   - failure/refusal routes and effect/closure laws;
   - projections and module mappings;
   - algorithmic obligations.

   U(B) must be empty, M(B) jointly satisfiable, and no non-equivalent networks admitted. Co-evolution is lawful only if this holds. Otherwise the design gate comes first. Section names or file presence do not establish completeness.
2. **Ontology evidence** (§7):
   - entity lifecycle matrix with every cell filled, `not_applicable` with a reason, or a `Gap:`;
   - authority matrix (proposer/evaluator/verifier/admitter/executor/projector/retirement);
   - transformation-admission relation for every consequential write or effect;
   - atomic/higher-order function derivation;
   - whole-family Prime contraction.

   Thirteen FD operations should be contracted. They should not automatically become thirteen peer functions. Read operations (FD-003, FD-004, FD-011 projection) should be projections over one source truth where they are.
3. **Three views plus cross-view axiom evaluation.** The views are class, sequence and state. Every verdict is `pass`, `fail` or `not_applicable` with basis facts. An unresolved premise must not be recorded as `not_applicable`.
4. **Computational realisation projection (UP-019).** Commonised machinery receives semantic and realisation dispositions. Examples include closure computation (AC-R-008) reused by FD-004, FD-005 and FD-012, and digest/observation reused by FD-002, FD-007 and FD-012. No generic block may become a semantic authority.
5. **No interface bleed or proxy interface** (§12–13).
   - The CLI and MCP are thin projections of Ontology functions.
   - Neither reconstructs owner truth, turns projection into closure, or turns report/prompt logic into admission.
   - Neither forwards to an old path under a new name.
6. **Functor separation** (AC §5, AC-015/016).
   - Each `F_D[v]` binds exactly one traversal identity.
   - Named domain operations (acquire, render, serialise, dispatch, write) are not `F_D` aliases.
   - Any stop algebra Ω_v maps each value to exactly one core value, never `satisfied`.
   - Composition retains the separate F_P/F_D/F_H identities.
7. **Module lifecycle confirmation** (ASSURANCE §16). Each lifecycle phase is answered, declared not applicable with a reason, or recorded as a named gap with its owner.

## 5. Per-operation expectations

Each item gives the adequacy expectation, then the negative/control discriminators. An always-pass or always-refuse realisation must fail every discriminator (FDV-002).

**FD-001 Discover contracts**
- *Adequacy:* a versioned inventory with:
  - per-operation I/O contract;
  - supported method/profile bindings;
  - checked fragments;
  - effect/access prerequisites;
  - qualification scope;
  - explicit unsupported cases.

  The inventory selects no frame, role or next action.
- *Discriminators:*
  - an unknown operation, an unsupported method/profile and an ambiguous Product selection each refuse with the exact unmet contract;
  - the inventory does not imply qualification of unexercised variants.

**FD-002 Bind/observe workspace**
- *Adequacy:*
  - identities and digests for definition, method, subject, configuration and dependencies;
  - current bytes, including dirty and untracked content;
  - three distinct basis roles: builder rc.2, consumer method and represented subject;
  - observations stamped with scope and time;
  - reobservation before any dependent effect.
- *Discriminators:*
  - a HEAD match with changed dirty bytes must be detected (SC-003);
  - equal labels across bases create no join (SC-002);
  - inaccessible or ambiguous bindings report without substitution;
  - no "latest" is adopted.

**FD-003 Resolve records/source/grounding**
- *Adequacy:*
  - actual payload and source content returned with route, digest, grounding and coverage dispositions;
  - locator kept distinct from content;
  - search reports its scope;
  - Workers and Reviewers acquire raw deciding source directly.
- *Discriminators:*
  - missing, ambiguous, stale, wrong-basis and ungrounded references each return a named gap;
  - an inferred code edge or extraction confidence never authors a governing mapping (SC-004).

**FD-004 Dependencies/impact/coverage**
- *Adequacy:*
  - closure, path, impact and coverage computed over authored typed relations only;
  - computed facts kept separate from supplied F_P judgments and owner rulings;
  - predicate results only for a supported decidable contract.
- *Discriminators:*
  - applicability with true, false and missing premises maps per ACTIVATION §1: `satisfied` / `falsified` / `indeterminate` or `out_of_frame`, never `falsified` for a missing premise (SC-005);
  - an absent path under incomplete coverage yields no non-influence claim;
  - no frontier or re-entry choice is made.

**FD-005 Assemble/check context**
- *Adequacy:*
  - a projection of the caller-selected packet contract (RF §11), with no second packet schema;
  - a supplied-versus-to-acquire manifest;
  - declared closure and boundaries;
  - where a capacity is declared, the size measured in its unit over its stated boundary before the read, with unmeasured host context recorded;
  - fit judgment `indeterminate` without a complete-input bound;
  - `closure_unresolved` when Z, R, semantics or basis is inexact.
- *Discriminators:*
  - locator-only versus acquired input;
  - exceeded capacity, which must refine, sequence or refuse and never silently trim;
  - denied acquisition;
  - unknown host context;
  - closure or size success must not be reported as semantic sufficiency (SC-006).

**FD-006 Preflight contract**
- *Adequacy:*
  - findings bound to contract identity and digest, plus the Product-bound admission mechanism and its evidence;
  - drafted and admitted states retained separately;
  - durable result surface required;
  - missing surface yields `admission_refused(authority)`.
- *Discriminators:*
  - ticket, `active` lane, role name or a shape check does not mint admission (SC-007);
  - a self-drafted contract is not admitted;
  - basis-unsupported input yields `admission_refused(basis)`.

**FD-007 Dispatch/notify/observe**
- *Adequacy:*
  - invocation identity, actual payload and settings;
  - acknowledgments, output and partial output, effect and termination observations;
  - precondition recheck before the effect;
  - effects recorded as `completed` / `not_effected` / `completion_unknown`, with the end of the effect tracked separately;
  - event-driven notifications;
  - fallback observation bounded by supplied conditions and not a polling controller.
- *Discriminators:*
  - a lost response or timeout stays `completion_unknown`;
  - a cancel request does not prove rollback;
  - a changed precondition is exposed, not silently rebased (SC-003, SC-008);
  - an unadmitted dispatch refuses.

**FD-008 Capture closed return**
- *Adequacy:*
  - raw return and provenance preserved;
  - binding, format and currentness findings;
  - only actually returned evaluations, with predicate and domain outcome recorded.
- *Discriminators* (SC-009):
  - stale and wrong-subject/basis returns are held as gaps per FOUNDATIONS Stale results, not relabelled `invalid_basis` for the composite;
  - duplicate returns are not counted as independent;
  - conflicting returns are preserved;
  - no result is fabricated for a not-performed evaluation;
  - schema validity is not assessment;
  - transport failure is reported separately.

**FD-009 Review evidence preparation**
- *Adequacy:*
  - independent acquisition routes to authority, candidate, tests/fixtures and proof;
  - byte/record diffs;
  - assertion provenance kept separate from execution provenance;
  - expectation recording before candidate exposure;
  - an exposure record.
- *Discriminators* (SC-010):
  - an author summary or fresh author session is not certified independent;
  - the tool outputs no severity, verdict or disposition;
  - a Reviewer needs no author-only privilege.

**FD-010 Declared checks/joins**
- *Adequacy:*
  - `F_D[v]` judgments only for evaluated properties;
  - joins only under a supplied qualified operator;
  - FOUNDATIONS conjunction rules preserved: the three inputs, gap kinds, survival, predicates travel and reported value.
- *Discriminators* (SC-011):
  - a necessary-condition pass does not satisfy a stronger predicate;
  - wrong-basis input is preserved;
  - not-performed and uncovered gaps keep the conjunction short of `satisfied`;
  - all-satisfied with a remaining gap yields `indeterminate`;
  - unsupported calculus operations refuse.

**FD-011 Project/record**
- *Adequacy:*
  - source-linked projections of existing carriers;
  - authorised writes only through a named Writer, with preimage, grant and conditions checked;
  - applied record or refusal returned;
  - original ruling wording kept separate from interpretation;
  - verdict, recommendation, disposition, checkpoint, closure and release kept distinct.
- *Discriminators* (SC-012):
  - an Executive-frame or Reviewer mutation refuses where EXEC is selected;
  - a recommendation is not adopted as a disposition;
  - no rival ledger is created;
  - a view does not own state.

**FD-012 Refresh/handoff**
- *Adequacy:*
  - S_Δ computed from D*(t) ∩ I(Δ), with the three invalidators;
  - `retain_applicable` used only with established validity conditions;
  - a reconstructable handoff;
  - partial writes retained;
  - uncertain effects reconciled before dependent action.
- *Discriminators* (SC-013):
  - an absent edge does not imply unaffectedness;
  - a session or role change alone invalidates nothing;
  - frozen studies and original judgments are not rewritten onto new subjects;
  - no blind retry.

**FD-013 Attempt/usage evidence**
- *Adequacy:*
  - units and boundaries recorded;
  - elapsed versus summed intervals;
  - partial and failed attempts;
  - unknown counters stay unknown;
  - reproducible aggregation without double counting;
  - anticipated costs kept separate from measurements.
- *Discriminators* (SC-014):
  - no universal quota;
  - fewer calls is not a lower-cost claim.

**FDV-001** requires one real bounded task across all three roles, using ordinary documented interfaces, with the four ASSURANCE §13 claims kept distinct. Unexercised operations remain gaps.

**FDV-002** requires paired positive and negative cases, with assertion and execution provenance, resumable from durable sources.

## 6. Authority, trust and boundary expectations (concrete)

- **Authority:** a tool holds no semantic, admission, decision, acceptance or publication authority. Its effects run only under a supplied exact operation grant, subject and territory. The design's authority matrix must name the owner of each tool effect separately from the invoking actor (DESIGN_MODULE Authority model). Composition must not widen authority.
- **Trust:** internal location does not establish trust. Untrusted inputs, mutable state and effect checks required by the supported threat model are retained (EXEC §10). Embedded instructions in acquired content are data.
- **Executor:** actor, executor, domain operation and functor kind are separate coordinates (AC §5). The deterministic wrapper does not make F_P output deterministic or semantic.
- **Preconditions and effects:** preconditions are rechecked before each effect. Effects use the ACTIVATION §3 Effects refinement. Resumption is not idempotent.
- **Current state:** the workspace is the truth surface and an account is not. Observations are distinct from immutable authority and history (ASSURANCE §13 obligation 4).
- **Source boundary:** only the exact selected basis is acquired. A stale derived surface is not used. A matching digest does not establish fidelity.
- **Closure boundary:** closure must be finite and unique, otherwise `closure_unresolved`.
- **Fit boundary:** measured only where a capacity is declared.
- **Provenance:** preserved through every join.
- **Refusal:** each refusal states its cause and maps to exactly one core value. A refusal is not recorded as a step's stop.

## 7. Direct Executive/Worker/Reviewer usability

- Interfaces must be usable by all three roles without private study knowledge (PRODUCT; FD-001).
- Workers get direct implementation and proof context. Their read scope may exceed their write territory (EXEC §9).
- Reviewers get direct candidate, test and source access under exposure control.
- Executive use includes no mutation path when the EXEC profile is selected (EXEC §6). An Executive's inspection does not become a Reviewer result (§17).
- A design that routes Worker or Reviewer acquisition only through Executive-assembled packets fails this criterion.
- A design review can assess **contract sufficiency** only (ASSURANCE §13). It cannot assess user usability.

## 8. Method-owned result, conjunction and reuse distinctions

The tools must reproduce, and must not redefine, the following:

- the five core values;
- the refinement rules:
  - each refined value maps to exactly one core value;
  - cause becomes part of the value where it changes the mapping;
  - a child value keeps its parent's core value;
  - no mapping raises standing;
- availability, assessment and acceptance as three separate results;
- `incomplete` mapping to `falsified` only for availability;
- not performed, which has no result, kept distinct from `indeterminate`;
- the ambiguity and effect refinements;
- disposition values limited to `accept`, `local_repair`, `re_enter` and `reject`;
- exclusion meaning "not evaluated", never "met";
- a `satisfied` obtained via `not_applicable` counting only toward the declared disjunction;
- reuse under the three invalidators;
- a reused judgment never satisfying a required independent judgment.

Repricing dispositions are recorded states, not results.

## 9. Decision-complete design and thin public projections

The design must show:

- every CLI/MCP operation traced to an Ontology function and its authority role;
- every sequence message bound to a declared transform or effect;
- state diagrams covering refusal, blocked, retry, escalation and terminal states;
- that raw probabilistic output cannot transition directly to accepted or closed.

Protocol and schema choices are HOW, but they must introduce no semantic peer. An existing script or worker launcher may be consumed as a building block. It is not retrospectively qualified, and it is not a proxy for a real operation.

## 11. Assertion versus execution provenance

- Every expected result traces along the chain: ticket → triage → re-entry → requirement/design → expectation (TICKETS Test case authority). It is never derived from implementation behaviour.
- Execution records alone are insufficient.
- Agreement between design-derived and implementation-derived results may reflect a shared error. Disagreement requires diagnosis.
- Same-process proofs do not establish cross-process or durable authority (ACTIVATION §7).

## 12. What a design review can assess versus later claims

**A design review can assess:**
- decision-completeness;

**Not assessable by design review:**

- path realisation;
- ordinary user usability;
- harnessed or live UAT;

Under ASSURANCE §13, design-only work reports none of these as satisfied. They remain unclaimed or unresolved.
