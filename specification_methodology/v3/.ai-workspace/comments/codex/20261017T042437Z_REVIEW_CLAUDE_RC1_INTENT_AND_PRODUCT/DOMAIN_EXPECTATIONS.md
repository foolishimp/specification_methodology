## Governing expectations

1. **Purpose and lawful construction.** Profiles must advance the same A-to-Z construction relation, retain selected purpose and constraints, and distinguish admissibility, preservation, useful progress, completion and acceptance. No local success, valid syntax, or resource exhaustion establishes the outer outcome. Sources: INTENT 5–17, 21–41; PRODUCT 66–82; requirements/README 103–118.
2. **All ten pillar contributions.** The reconstruction must provide actual deciding laws for specification structure; world models and identity; algebraic operations; functional design; outcome-driven transitions; information/interpretation; frames; computational binding; evidence/correction/authority; and axiomatic representation. No individual pillar substitutes for all the others, and finite capacity alone cannot derive identity, purpose or authority. Sources: PRODUCT 35–52; PRINCIPLES 7–104; requirements/README 38–73.
3. **Singular shared law with domain binding.** Each generic determination has one owner and is usable without an optional profile. Profiles supply necessary additional meanings and mappings, then apply the core at real entry and deciding points. A link is insufficient if the core delegates a required condition back to the domain. No precedence rule cures competing owners. Sources: PRODUCT 84–105, 127–138; requirements/README 133–174.
4. **Typed functional design and execution freedom.** Domain decomposition preserves interfaces, invariants, material failure cases, effects and composition compatibility. Operation kind, actor identity and execution mechanism are separate. A human execution of an otherwise deterministic operation does not inherently require human adjudication. A human or mixed process remains a permitted computational binding; code, events, full-history reconstruction, fixed hierarchy and ticket entry cannot become generic prerequisites without applicable obligations. Sources: PRODUCT 41–50, 107–113; requirements/README 89–101, 120–131; CAPABILITIES 21, 34.
5. **Mathematical standing.** A claimed category defines objects, arrows, arrow equality, identities and associative composition. A claimed functor maps objects and arrows and establishes identity/composition preservation. A mathematical label does not discharge its laws; absent demonstration the claim is a hypothesis. Exact and approximate transformations retain their respective meaning obligations, including a bound for the complete approximate composition. Formal notation, structural validation and sampled tests support different claims. Sources: INTENT 37–41; PRODUCT 140–179; requirements/README 178–195; ASSURANCE 7–23, 123–129.
6. **World, representation and observed state.** Subject identity, representation identity, current state, historical observations, governing conditions and predictions remain distinct. Equal bytes do not identify two subjects; unchanged identity does not authenticate new content. A bounded domain object does not by itself establish state sufficiency, conditional independence, temporal Markov behavior or a mathematical Markov category. Sources: PRODUCT 42, 56–80; PRINCIPLES 31–41; ASSURANCE 25–56.
7. **One result algebra.** Each material evaluation/decision carries its predicate, original domain outcome and exactly one core result with enough conditions to determine that result. Domain outcomes cannot erase known failure, unknown truth, nonperformance, invalid basis or uncertain effects. Candidate availability is not fitness or acceptance. Conjunction and composition belong to the core. Sources: PRODUCT 127–138; requirements/README 155–176.
8. **Actual fidelity and observation binding.** Interpretation, source, encoding basis, carrier and checker identities stay distinct. A representation must preserve permission as well as obligation, uncertainty and exceptions. A checker result needs both a justified assertion and a supported observation relation to the actual subject; identical observations of cases with opposite predicate truth cannot support a complete decision. Sources: requirements/README 75–87; A_C_DEPENDENCY 5–12, 37–43, 61–93; ASSURANCE 25–70.
9. **Correction and continued work.** Domain recovery diagnoses realization, governing requirement/design, prediction and observation separately, reacquires changed reality, preserves valid work and resolves material unknown effects before dependent actions. A changed model cannot erase its counterexample. Sources: PRODUCT 68–82; requirements/README 97, 110–114; ASSURANCE 97–121.
10. **Scope and evidence limits.** Full written Product obligations and the owner's presently selected baseline are distinct. Complete a_c coverage, remaining actual-use expectation sets and authority compressions/projections are deferred under the activation's c5209f00 scope; their absence alone is not a fresh baseline defect. Wrong present claims or bindings still are. Desk traces do not establish empirical task-context fit, global reduction or complete independent qualification. Sources: PRODUCT 319–321, 419–427; activation scope.

## Independent discriminator cases

| Case | Expected decision before candidate inspection |
|---|---|
| A clerk totals an invoice by a completely specified arithmetic rule, then a designated person adjudicates an exception | Preserve deterministic operation versus required human judgment; both can run in a human environment. No machine-only inference from function kind. |
| A human workshop carries a bounded design change on paper under an admitted mandate, without code or an event-stream runtime | Permit this binding when governing obligations are satisfied; preserve any actual independent review and effect requirements. |
| A deterministic two-step transformation has a successful first step whose output violates the second step's precondition | Refuse the composition or acquire/repair the missing premise; local successes cannot certify the end-to-end outcome. |
| An optional traversal-history profile is not selected for an otherwise valid finite-state process | Generic conformance remains decidable through core law; no compulsory adoption of optional occurrence IDs or history machinery. |
| A well-typed map sends an identity arrow to a nonidentity or fails to preserve a declared composition | Do not call it a demonstrated functor merely because map typing is valid. |
| Two approximate steps each meet a local tolerance but amplify error in composition | Withhold the end-to-end adequacy claim until the actual composition bound is established. |
| Two physical accounts have identical export bytes; one account later changes while keeping its identifier | Keep subject identity, representation equality and freshness separate. |
| A bounded inventory object excludes a material replenishment history that changes its next-state distribution | Boundedness alone cannot support the stronger Markov claim; expose the missing state/premise or narrow the claim. |
| A projection says `ready`, while the actual subject has either a failed obligation or an effect of unknown completion | Preserve the exact assessed predicate and unresolved effect/failure through the core result; `ready` cannot promote completion or fitness. |
| A human checker sees the same receipt in one delivered and one undelivered case | Receipt-only observation cannot establish full delivery; require further evidence, narrower scope or an indeterminate result. |
| A UI projection is usable and well-typed but omits a required authority distinction in its action path | Representation compliance does not grant the prohibited action; retain the governing permission at the actual consumer. |
| A changed source representation has valid structure but silently turns an optional policy into a mandatory precondition | Treat as fidelity and permission loss; structural checks cannot qualify that change. |
