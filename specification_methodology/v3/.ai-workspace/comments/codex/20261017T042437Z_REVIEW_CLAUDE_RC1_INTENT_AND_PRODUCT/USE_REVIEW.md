## U1 — Medium: a generic migration ticket imports optional recurrence policy

**Governing obligation.** Common `requirements/README.md:139–151` assigns each determination one owning locus, gives profiles only their added domain or operational choices, requires consumers to refer to or faithfully refine that owner, and requires generic determinations to remain available without adopting an optional profile. In particular, line 151 distinguishes a generic obligation from additional policy that applies when a profile is selected. The candidate also makes this distinction: `standards/PROFILES.md:31–49` limits profiles to their additional bindings and choices; `standards/PRODUCT_DEFINITION.md:665–693` distinguishes availability, adoption and activation.

**Candidate loci.** `standards/TICKETS.md:3–24` declares a tier-2 method owner and expressly includes implementation-migration declaration, checklist and review rules. The migration category at `425–438` is not restricted to adoption of DESIGN_MODULE. At `858–883`, an implementation-migration ticket must declare its library/commonisation choice; the second credible recurrence forces a review, and a third local rebuild blocks implementation unless the ticket consumes or extends a reusable surface, or links an explicit do-not-commonise decision. There is no adoption condition on this rule. Lines 866–867 explicitly identify `DESIGN_MODULE §10 Recurrence extraction` as its owner.

That cited owner is an optional profile: `standards/DESIGN_MODULE.md:3–13` includes recurrence among its profile realisation rules; `1917–1944` owns the rule of two and calls a third rebuild nonconformant to the **design method**, with TICKETS owning ticket-level recording/enforcement. `2395–2404` reserves adoption to Product authority, `2406–2429` applies the profile when adopted, and `2431–2433` permits explicit partial adoption by boundary and rule family. `standards/PROFILES.md:73–89` identifies it as the software-design profile.

**Concrete counterexample.** A software Product adopts the core but explicitly does not adopt DESIGN_MODULE or its recurrence rule family. Under its accepted design, an admitted migration ticket replaces one bounded authoritative path. The actor has the correct work/effect grant, satisfies the Product's actual contract, and creates the third instance of an authority-neutral helper pattern. Its existing law requires no library extraction review or do-not-commonise ADR. Applying `TICKETS:868–877` nevertheless blocks implementation unless the actor performs one of those extra policy acts. Applying the optional owner's adoption boundary leaves that unadopted policy inapplicable. The same work therefore has incompatible applicability answers at the owner and generic consumer.

**Why this survives the obvious defenses.** Calling the ticket procedure the recording/enforcement owner does not grant it ownership of the profile's applicability. The profile expressly owns the design-review obligation. A reference from a mandatory core member is not Product adoption, and availability in the cut is explicitly insufficient. Partial adoption makes the case sharper: a Product could adopt another DESIGN_MODULE rule family while omitting recurrence. Nor is the block merely advice: the consumer says the third rebuild “blocks implementation.” General purpose principles do not supply the numeric second/third policy.

**Impact and severity.** Medium is a qualitative consequence grade here: this rule can block otherwise lawful bounded construction and require extra design work under policy the Product did not select. It compromises a stated core/profile permission and creates unnecessary interpretation at the operating consumer. It does not establish data loss, an authority escalation permitting effects, a universal failure of all migration work, or observed frequency. Trigger probability and deployed incidence were not measured. Confidence in the source contradiction is high; assigning Product priority or promotion effect remains the decision owner's act.

**Smallest repair.** At the TICKETS consumer, make enforcement conditional on adoption of this rule family for the affected boundary, retaining the generic migration declaration and the selected profile's existing owner. If the owner instead intends recurrence to be mandatory method law for every migration, it must deliberately assign that generic obligation and its policy to a core owner and reconcile the profile/adoption contract; that is a larger governing change. Re-read the nonadopted and partially adopted cases after repair. No new test framework is needed to establish this prose-level correction.
