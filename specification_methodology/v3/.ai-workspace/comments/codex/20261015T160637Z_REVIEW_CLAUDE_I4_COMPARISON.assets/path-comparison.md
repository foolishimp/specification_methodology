## Foundations, consumers and reduction

The architecture difference is substantive. Claude places practical transitions, configuration, effects and reuse in `ACTIVATION`; evaluation policy in `EVALUATION`; and result values/conjunction in `FOUNDATIONS`. Its family tables expose premises, consequences, retained choices and consuming sections. The inspected RF-012, AC-007, review and Ticket consumers actually call the shared result or reuse owner (`C-frame:352–366,581–588`; `C-calculus:513–545`; `C-evaluation:195–208`; `C-ticket:1153–1187`). These are positive ownership-and-use witnesses, beyond an introductory foundations paragraph.

Codex places current state, results and configuration in `FOUNDATIONS`, with shared application, projection and recovery in `TRANSFORMATION_LIBRARY`. Bind/Acquire/Transform/Evaluate/Conjoin/Dispose are conditional responsibilities, not compulsory stages. Its application owner distinguishes admission from outcome success, retains domain policies and grants, and is invoked by the inspected Spec/Ticket consumers (`X-library:70–113,115–180`; `X-triage:1431–1439,1521–1528`; `X-ticket:1042–1063`). This creates a more compact common vocabulary at those consumers, while its additional indirection still needs sufficiency evidence for real users.

The retained differences have purposes: Claude keeps domain-selected ordering of nonsatisfied results; Codex explicitly separates performance and normalizes observed effect parts. A shared result type does not remove the need to bind a predicate, outcome meaning, mandatory set, observation boundary and authority. Neither design should receive extra credit merely for its labels. This review does not accept changed predecessor meanings or replace the missing owner decisions.

The baseline sample rules out an exaggerated I4 reduction claim. RC2 already made Ticket triage consume Spec's computed classification (`P-ticket:600–622`, `P-spec:1251–1324`).
