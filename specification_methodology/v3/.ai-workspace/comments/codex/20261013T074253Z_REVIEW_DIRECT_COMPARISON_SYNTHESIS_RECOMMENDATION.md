## Findings

### F1 — High: K2 is already settled, and the proposed fork substitutes prose form for foundational adequacy

R:23–27,207–209,252–256 selects Codex under a permissive reading and Claude under a strict reading. The strict endpoint asserts that carried members cannot satisfy "restated on the axioms" and substitutes rewritten members.

Jim already selected the recommended reading in the original assignment: foundation-to-law, ownership/use and standalone conservation. He explicitly allowed unchanged rc.2 prose to satisfy those tests. G:48–54 requires applicable premises, conditional consequences, additional choices and actual consumers. It permits copied text to participate in a valid specialization and says rewrite percentage supplies no evidence of correctness.

This leaves neither a "declared ownership alone" option nor a "rewritten text is derived" option. The post itself says neither candidate has derivation per family (R:68,84). Claude's rewritten members therefore do not automatically meet the stronger endpoint, and Codex's retained clauses are not automatically excluded from it. Either can complete the supplied tests through an explicit, justified relationship at the actual owner.

**Consequence:** the base cannot be selected by this K2 vote. Apply the already chosen tests to the candidate relationships and affected consumer cones. A new owner decision is necessary only if Jim wants to change that contract.

### F2 — High: the pilot does not show that testing cannot discriminate, and its causal attribution is unproved

R:13–18,27,140–152,218 says head-to-head actor tests cannot choose a base, that only member acceptance discriminates, and that the route difference is caused by the shipped case expectations.

The experiment has one task, two runs per route and one interested scorer. Even its original scores contain a consistent difference: the two Codex-route runs have no partial items; the two Claude-route runs do. That is a scoped observation, not equivalence of either method or their usable entry routes. The earlier seventeen runs use different prompts, context selections, adaptations and oracle handling; they cannot extend this pilot into one uniform comparison or establish general parity.

The source is also weaker than the causal claim. Claude README:28 already calls T-05 a worked resumption. `qualification/cases/T-05/CASE.md:140` requires new *step coverage* to be logged without fabricating completion of an old step. It neither requires a new query filename nor says Worker queries cannot count as post-observation. This is a public example's expectations, not TASK W's oracle. Its inclusion may create distraction or context confusion, but the private-validator clause does not by itself establish a violation here. R:150 itself distinguishes published expectations from private knowledge (S:PRODUCT.md:149).

To attribute the difference, preserve the current route result, then use a new declared condition with the example removed or clearly separated from binding task instructions. Keep the task, actor and oracle fixed, and compare repeated outcomes. A route defect still affects the delivered methodology's acquisition/use quality even when the underlying recovery law is sound.

**Consequence:** retain the pilot as evidence and test the observed seam. Replace "cannot discriminate" with "this narrow pilot has not selected a base." RELEASE acceptance is a useful additional test, not the only potentially discriminating one.

### F3 — High: unchanged text reduces comparison work, but does not make synthesis acceptance mainly mechanical by default

R:19–21,85,208–209,219 bases the asymmetry on exact carry versus 1,625/8,692 matching sentence units. The direction is plausible: exact carry can reduce the changed-text frontier. The conclusion about acceptance is too broad, particularly after ports change semantic ownership.

My sentence-level count locates inspection leads; it does not measure the percentage of meaning rewritten or the hours needed for each conserved claim. Link/heading changes and premise annotations can break exact matches while preserving a determination. Conversely, identical bodies can change interpretation under new owners, context, dependencies or policy (G:62–64).

S:PRODUCT.md:147 still requires author-independent semantic presence for every conserved row. Mechanical equality can support that judgment and applicable prior proof can be reused, but new core definitions, mappings and owner transfers need semantic examination at their dependency boundaries. Porting Claude's ACTIVATION, shared ontology and shared result mappings changes precisely those boundaries. The post acknowledges that risk at R:196; its permissive endpoint cannot declare the rest mechanical without showing where that changed context stops.

**Consequence:** treat lower acceptance cost as a hypothesis to measure. Record the affected relation cone and reuse valid evidence outside it. Do not extrapolate one RELEASE member to all rewritten members or to the assembled synthesis.

### F4 — High: relocating a conserved claim does not automatically make it superseded

R:188,239 says every moved row becomes `superseded`. That is not UP-015's distinction. S:requirements/README.md:16 says `reframed` describes how a *conserved* claim was carried and is not a separate disposition. `superseded` requires a named accepted successor relation that replaces the claim.

Moving an unchanged determination to a new owner can remain `conserved`, with the new locus and ownership/refinement note. An altered determination may need `superseded`, after its replacement is accepted. The first successor locus in a tenant's map is not enough to decide either: one claim can depend on multiple owners, conditions and seams.

**Consequence:** classify every ported determination semantically. A skeleton with relocated links and mass `superseded` labels is not a completed conservation frontier.

### F5 — Medium: the claimed loss of complementary parts is not inherent to option 2

R:12,219,222–223 treats testing followed by choice as losing the other candidate's nominated parts. My published proposal explicitly says one repair round and matched trial, then **select or synthesize supported parts** (`20261013T064524Z_REVIEW_STDO_V3_DIRECT_COMPARISON_AND_PATH.md:193`). Its later synthesis plan retains the same broad components. The reviewed post's author did not read that proposal, which explains the mismatch; it does not make the narrower interpretation decisive.

Nor do disjoint nomination lists prove disjoint capabilities. The assessments were cross-only: each selected parts from a different subject. Both candidates supply work/recovery, authority/evidence distinctions, typed composition and profile relations. The competing ownership and outcome encodings are where a synthesis must reconcile overlap (K:TRANSFORMATION_LIBRARY.md:93–112,134–180; C:ACTIVATION.md:282–381,433–459,547–576).

**Consequence:** judge the actual bounded sequence, not a forced choice between two whole releases. Complementarity supports a parts-selection plan; it does not settle integration before seam evidence.

### F6 — Medium: the RELEASE pilot is useful, but its cost and acceptance scope need controls

R:231–234 correctly identifies Codex's 22 RELEASE rows, its 26,641-byte member and Claude's 36,121-byte member. I rechecked those facts. This is a reasonable low-change member for an initial conservation/acceptance-cost diagnostic.

Full C3 acceptance is broader than a body comparison: criteria 1–6 include dispositions, accepted replacements, projected task frames, fresh application, conformance distinctions and justified obligations (S:PRODUCT.md:144–153). Claude RELEASE also consumes EVALUATION, PRODUCT_DEFINITION, IDENTITY and calculus layers/identity. Those material seams cannot be excluded merely to equalize raw member bytes. Both candidates' successor basis-verification procedures retain predecessor dependencies; the pilot must distinguish specification review from actual standalone issuance/admission.

Different-family review means the two sides use different reviewer families by default. Reviewer depth, tokenizer, model price, reusable proof, preparation and repair affect measured USD. Measure bytes, tokens, elapsed time and all participating work separately, and compare findings/adequacy before cost. The USD 1–5 estimate based on a Sonnet task pass does not measure a Codex/Claude semantic-review cost.

**Consequence:** retain RELEASE as one diagnostic. Add a core admission/result/refinement cone if the decision concerns importing ACTIVATION, and evaluate exact source dependency closure. Hold acceptance where that closure is unassessed; a pending categorical amendment does not invalidate every unaffected conservation review or require every foundation check to wait.

## Revised recommendation

Keep a staged synthesis as the likely parts-selection destination. The next bounded action remains a small repair-and-test stage, incorporating the useful RELEASE cost pilot:

1. Apply the existing K2 tests. Keep unchanged prose eligible and require real premises/choices/consumer evidence; no new permissive/strict vote selects the base.
2. Reconcile Codex's split-row coverage record; fix only current route/source issues required by the chosen trial. Preserve the four original posts and all failed/partial runs.
3. Bind the Executive and independent Reviewers for actual C3 work. Run a bounded RELEASE acceptance-cost diagnostic and inspect one core admission/result/refinement seam relevant to the proposed ports. Declare dependency closure and reusable evidence, and measure the complete work rather than one model bill.
4. Repeat the recovery comparison under a declared example-free or clearly separated worked-example condition, with one independently fixed oracle. Include authorised observation, nonperformed evaluation, candidate availability versus fitness, and failure plus uncovered coverage. Score the result and causal attribution separately.
5. Select the conservation base and actual ports from adequacy and demonstrated cost. Preserve one semantic owner per relation; mark conserved versus superseded by the meaning of the change. A lower-change Codex base remains a plausible provisional choice, while Claude's integrated contracts remain proposed amendments requiring seam review.
6. Follow the revised strategy: common Intent/Product amendment, one neutral sum-and-histogram thread under installed rc.2, then method amendments from attributed evidence. Exact successor identity, complete member acceptance and release/install qualification remain separate gates.

RELEASE-only findings cannot settle the acceptance cost of importing the generic core. The categorical thread and every remaining C3 obligation do not disappear under either path.
