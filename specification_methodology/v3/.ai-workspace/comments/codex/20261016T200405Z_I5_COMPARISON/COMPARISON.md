# STDO v3 I5: reciprocal comparison and recommended basis

## Decision

**Continue from Codex as the better current working basis.** It has the more complete current I5 package, reproducible interpretation and explicit source/evidence bindings. Its shared application contract works at the sampled consumers. Claude has the clearer ordinary task index and a useful separation of activation, evaluation and assurance.

The evidence does **not** establish an intrinsically superior methodology. Three fresh native review cones favor Codex as the current basis; the fresh Claude reviewer, after source readback corrected several initial claims, finds no substantive semantic winner. My recommendation therefore rests principally on usable current integration and the smaller remaining publication gap, with some positive sampled design evidence. It is not a claim of reviewer consensus, better practical performance or statistically independent agreement.

This is a recommendation about where to continue construction. **Neither candidate qualifies for acceptance.** Neither has completed the six member criteria, the shared actual-use experiment or the reserved replacement decisions. Under the common Candidate Comparison rule the qualified-selection result remains **no qualified choice**. No candidate, requirement or release was changed by this review.

## Corrections made during reciprocal review

The [original fresh Claude report](CROSSFAMILY_FINAL.md) is preserved. [Source triangulation](TRIANGULATION.md) rejected four advertised conflicts: the failure-A/missing-B conjunction example; incompatibility between four-cause diagnosis and layer-based change classes; fabricated performance in Codex's missing-check records; and a compulsory software realization in the cited Claude clauses. The actual sources preserve the relevant distinctions. The subsequent [informed Claude readback](CROSSFAMILY_READBACK_FINAL.md) also rejects those advertised conflicts and further narrows the progress claim after locating the explicit §5 owner reference. It treats the observation-consumer finding as ambiguous and the ledger finding as smaller in scope. Those qualifications govern this synthesis; the original reports remain available unchanged.

The original semantic report also classified its consumer counterexamples under criterion 2 too broadly. That criterion requires comparison with the relevant predecessor claim and disposition. Those comparisons were not performed in that cone. The supplement corrects criterion 2 to unresolved; the concrete common-source counterexamples instead bear on criterion 5 and, where applicable, criterion 6.

The informed readback changes the fresh Claude recommendation from Codex to **no substantive preference established**. It did not re-evaluate the native acquisition/publication or evidence cone. I accept its correction to the progress ownership claim and retain the observation ambiguity explicitly; those claims cannot bear a stronger semantic ranking.

These corrections matter to the choice. Codex is not preferred because Claude supposedly loses known failures, because the common law mandates Codex's priority order, or because either candidate supposedly cannot route a prediction/observation defect.
