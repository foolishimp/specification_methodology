## Findings

### S1 — Claude's observation consumer requires more discrimination than its owner

**Severity: medium.** This can require extra evidence or an unnecessary hold for an already decidable obligation. It does not create false success in the demonstrated case.

The owner gives the correct condition: equal observations must imply equal truth values of the checked predicate (`L:FOUNDATIONS.md:1287-1302`). The actual assurance consumer instead says that where the observation cannot separate two admissible subjects, the frame returns further evidence, a narrower scope or `indeterminate` (`L:ASSURANCE.md:670-686`, especially 681-683). It omits the condition that the two cases differ on the predicate. The common law requires that condition (`B:requirements/ASSURANCE.md:49-56`); it does not require an injective observation of all subject detail.

Concrete counterexample: let an admissible state be `(required_fact, irrelevant_annotation)`, with the first coordinate Boolean and the second either A or B. The observation returns the first coordinate, and the obligation is that this coordinate is true. The observation merges distinct states but perfectly decides the obligation. A checker that returns satisfied for true and falsified for false is sound and complete on all four cases. The owner permits that checker; the consumer's unqualified trigger asks for more evidence or a narrower scope despite the adequate observation.

`SEMANTICS_assets/observation_counterexample.py` and its JSON output enumerate those four cases. All owner-sufficiency, satisfied-soundness, falsified-soundness and complete-decision checks are true; distinct subjects still share an observation. This is an independent finite interpretation witness, not an execution of either candidate or a universal proof.

The corresponding Codex consumer binds the actual predicate, narrower claims and observations needed to distinguish **material cases**, and applies the owner without an all-subject-separation condition (`C:STDO_REFERENCE_FRAME_BASELINE.md:1244-1252`; `C:FOUNDATIONS.md:684-696`). Claude's linked owner makes the intended interpretation recoverable, mitigating likely harm, but a reference does not make the stronger procedural wording disappear. A sufficient correction would bind the consumer's ambiguity to cases with opposite truth values for the claimed predicate. The table shorthand “a checker is sound only where its observation separates the cases” at `L:FOUNDATIONS.md:1158` should be checked at the same time: the owner distinguishes soundness from always deciding.

### S2 — Codex's generic construction assessor still names software unconditionally

**Severity: medium, localized scope/wording defect.** The consequence is false blocking or pressure to introduce an unnecessary software artifact for a permitted human or mixed realization.

Codex explicitly permits human and mixed bindings (`C:SPEC_METHOD.md:107-109,908-933`), identifies code as the software case of Realisation (`1113-1119`), and makes DMM and event-based observation conditional in the ordinary method (`3352-3372`). The construction family itself says human, machine and mixed bindings retain the same affected obligations (`1818-1833`). Yet its promotion consumer directs the independent assessor to evaluate “the exact live code, authority paths, proof, and installed subject” without an applicability qualification (`1886-1894`).

A mixed realization can use an agent to construct a manual operating procedure, then have people execute it and an independent person inspect the procedure and direct observations. It has a real realization and required evidence but no code to inspect. The unqualified software noun is an extra required input on that admitted path. Common `B:requirements/README.md:124-131` requires generic procedures and their projections to permit the human binding without Code, Events or other unselected machinery.

Claude's acquired counterpart applies generic steps and execution environments in `L:SPEC_METHOD.md:1626-1659,1792-1808`, and its restart contract explicitly allows files, an event stream or another admitted owner as carrier choices (`L:ACTIVATION.md:1372-1388`). No mandatory-code input was found in those matched consumers. Its design-to-module evidence route is conditional on the selected design profile (`L:ASSURANCE.md:930-936`).

The natural workaround is to read Codex's “code” as the actual bound realization. That is supported by its owners, but the consumer does not say it, and the common single-owner requirement does not resolve mismatched determinations by precedence (`B:requirements/README.md:146-151`). A narrow wording correction at this consumer would remove the issue. No broader design rewrite is implied.

### S3 — Claude overstates the derivation of an append-only ledger

**Severity: low for current operational behavior; material weakness in the sampled reduction argument.** The profile policy remains available, but its claimed foundational necessity is unsupported.

`L:WORLD_MODEL.md:178` puts “the attribute ledger is append-only” in the consequence of P4b, P6, P3 and I1. The instance premises (`163-172`) do not require an append-only ledger. P4b requires the needed step-produced facts to be recorded (`L:FOUNDATIONS.md:495-509`); identity and justification preserve exact subjects and support. These premises establish conservation and recoverability, not a unique update architecture.

A mutable current attribute index plus authenticated retained snapshots/evidence can preserve every required prior fact, source relation and immutable published cut while allowing the index to replace rows. This satisfies the cited premises but not an append-only-current-ledger policy. Append-only can still be a valid additional profile choice. Claude's choice column names Git/filesystem and storage topology, but leaves append-only in the asserted consequence; its surrounding account expressly claims to distinguish consequences from choices (`L:WORLD_MODEL.md:194-199`; `L:FOUNDATIONS.md:595-599`). The common reduction requirement requires that distinction (`B:requirements/README.md:62-66`).

Codex makes the separation explicitly on the matched decision: retaining the earlier claim's supporting basis is the consequence; append-only ledgers and publication/supersession rules are the member's additional policy (`C:WORLD_MODEL_METHOD.md:38`). This is a substantive argument advantage, not a count of tables. If a material additional premise requiring the particular ledger behavior is supplied, this finding should be revised. The acquired premise set supplies none. No claim is made that Claude's current ledger rule itself is an unauthorized new policy; replacement acceptance was not audited here.

## Comparative rationale and change-of-mind conditions

Codex is the stronger current semantic basis in this bounded comparison because its shared application contract supplies concrete deciding conditions, its consumer proof-selection clause directly protects the assertion-strength boundary, and its result record makes performance, predicate truth and observed effects independently inspectable. The exact result-conjunction consumer fixes mandatory claims and joins before the central rule; it cannot invent a competing priority or turn missing coverage into success. The ledger argument also demonstrates the desired distinction between necessary conservation and optional architecture. These are actual rule-to-consumer relations, not evidence that every candidate path has been reduced.

Claude's separation into ACTIVATION, EVALUATION and ASSURANCE is conceptually useful, and its explicit source-provenance clause and generic realization language deserve preservation if a later synthesis is authorized. Its flexible conjunction diagnostic priority is an explicit choice with retained failures, not an automatic defect. I do not infer that a more explicit tuple or a fixed priority is the only lawful design. The current preference is therefore bounded and revisable.

I would reconsider or reverse it if (a) the Claude observation consumer is repaired to predicate-relative sufficiency and the ledger argument correctly declares its added policy; (b) matched current task returns demonstrate that its separated owners yield clearer or cheaper sufficient decisions without losing the required distinctions; and (c) the corresponding Codex path retains its generic-code inconsistency or reveals a stronger owner/consumer failure. Conversely, a Codex repair of S2 removes its demonstrated defect in this cone but supplies no missing qualification. An actual loss of known failure, effect uncertainty, human-process optionality, or required change-impact coverage would outweigh the present preference. Completing Claude's unfinished publication is relevant to readiness, but completion labels alone would not reverse the semantic judgment.
