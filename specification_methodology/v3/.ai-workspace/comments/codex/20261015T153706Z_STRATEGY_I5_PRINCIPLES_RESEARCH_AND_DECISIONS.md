## The synthesis and its ownership

The existing P1–P6 supply interpretation, purpose, identity/state, composition, finite capacity and justified claims/authority. The construction model connects them; it adds no P7 and derives no purpose or permission from a resource limit. The existing pillars remain the contributions the method must account for.

The normative specification describes what must hold. A predictive or causal model describes how an intervention is expected to affect the world. Observations provide evidence about what actually happens. These relations need separate evaluations: an accurate prediction of failure does not make failure acceptable, and an incorrect prediction does not by itself prove that the required outcome failed. A requirement can be inadequate relative to its owning purpose; revising it still requires that owner's decision.

Strategy allocates resources among understanding, planning, building, observing and checking. The method demands a material rationale within the governing constraints, rather than exhaustive search for an optimum. Steel thread, phased work and iterative delivery can be assessed on that basis. A named strategy supplies neither permission nor proof of fitness.

A discrepancy returns through the same work mechanism as a feature. Diagnosis determines the affected owner and necessary evidence. Severity, priority and authority are distinct. A bug list is a view of existing work; the synthesis supplies no additional queue, ledger or actor sequence.

Recursion makes the distinction between preservation and progress unavoidable. A useful experiment can fail or expose more work. Continued action needs a supported purpose within its strategy and authority, while claims of convergence or termination need separate evidence. A stop preserves an unresolved outcome. An attempt that exhausts its resources has not thereby proved its goal impossible.

For example, if every permitted operation leaves an unsatisfactory state unchanged, adding feedback cannot make the destination reachable. This elementary counterexample is an author inference, not a theorem attributed to the control sources. It defeats a universal convergence claim without preventing lawful bounded investigation.
