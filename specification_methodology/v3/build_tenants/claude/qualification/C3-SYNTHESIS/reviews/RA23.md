## Findings

Severity: blocking / material / minor. None is blocking.

### Material, in the subjects

- **M1. REFERENCE_FRAME §1 Scope contradicts its own import table (#position, `conserved`).** rc.2 RF:64–67 bounds what the *standard owns*: "owns only its frame declaration, activation, evaluation, basis, relation, result, coverage, revision, and qualification concepts". REFERENCE_FRAME:70–74 rewrites this as what the *context holds*: "The context holds only [the nine kinds] concepts", and "A concept of these kinds that another member defines enters … by the imports below". The table at :97–99 then imports concepts outside the nine kinds: workspace, lens, role, persona; subject, outcome, evidence, actor, claim, judgment, residual; frame-set, semantic, operation and decision authority, actor identity. Read literally, these cannot be in the context, so a fresh actor may refuse to resolve "decision authority" there. rc.2 RF defines Authority, Workspace, Evidence and Role/persona in its own Definitions (rc.2 RF:101–321) under the same "owns only" sentence, so the nine kinds were always a loose bound on ownership. The defect is the rewrite from "owns" to "holds": it turns that loose ownership bound into a membership bound, and the import table then breaks the membership bound. The #position row (:1001) claims `conserved` on a changed determination. Smallest repair, in A3 territory: "This member owns only its own frame declaration, …, qualification concepts. The context also holds the concepts imported below, each kept by its owner."
- **M2. `translate` refinement is not total (E-5-3).** See row 3. Smallest repair: widen the cause at REFERENCE_FRAME:451 to "a refusal condition the translation declares, or an identity or equality mismatch under its declared equality law". Add `refusal(authority)`, mapped to `out_of_frame` as `activation_refusal(authority)` is (:443), naming the authority's owner.
