## Expectations (recorded before opening the subject)

Q1, transitions and configuration (R §Bounded Transitions 1, 4; P §Construction And Context Management ¶2–4; E-2-1, E-2-4 to E-2-7, E-2-9; E-7-10 to E-7-15; N-3, N-8; ledger A4a reading):

- X1.1 Construction binds the current state, the destination constraints and each transition's preservation obligations. Discovery may add obligations. A destination change goes through its owning authority and re-entry rule, and never establishes that the prior destination was reached. Completion needs evidence against the destination and its acceptance conditions. A stop retains its unresolved outcome. Completion is never inferred from an empty list or a count (E-7-10, E-7-11).
- X1.2 Items outside a coordination frame's current attention stay open; a bounded view never disposes of them (E-7-10).
- X1.3 Configuration is derived from operations, dependencies, actor capabilities and assurance needs. Its design explains the allocation with resource limits and failure assumptions. It may use one frame or many, with fixed or adaptive allocation. Generic text prescribes no role inventory, hierarchy or sequence (E-2-4, N-3). A directly authorised task that fits one sufficient frame may be activated directly, subject to any separately required assessment (E-7-12).
- X1.4 Decomposition, communication, authority and evaluation dependency stay distinct. Reconfiguration and redistribution keep dependencies, outstanding obligations and effects (an uncertain effect is resolved before dependent action), admitted authority and required independence. Self-organisation is no exemption (E-2-5, E-7-14).
- X1.5 Selection or naming of a frame grants nothing; each activation binds subject, result, capability and admitted authority (E-2-6, N-8; RF-006).
- X1.6 Independence rests on authorship and evidence conditions; a fresh context alone is not independence; shared common sources are allowed (E-2-7).
- X1.7 A configuration without a central Executive, or with unfamiliar responsibilities, is judged against task, dependencies, actor limits, authority and assurance, not by labels or topology; the selected installed configuration keeps its scope (E-7-13). Adequacy and optimality are separate; an optimality claim needs stated objectives, constraints, comparison scope and evidence (E-7-15).
- X1.8 Executive, Writer and Reviewer appear as one configuration. Where that configuration is selected, every predecessor grant, limit and prohibition stays in force. This includes the Executive's restriction against file or Git mutation (ledger A4a reading; P §Construction Authority), Reviewer no-repair and no self-assessment, Worker territory and the Executive drift locks.

- X3.1 The workspace and its authoritative inputs persist; the actor's context is a disposable, revisable account, not a second truth surface.

- X3.3 Discarding context resets nothing and does not prove an uncertain external effect absent.
- X3.4 Resumption starts from the current material state and keeps applicable valid work. It invalidates only affected claims, checking them even when bytes are unchanged, and resolves unknown effects before dependent action. There is no universal reset or full-history replay, and handoffs use closed results, not private context.
