## 1. Lawfulness of identity use (facts 1 to 4)

**Fact 1: unlawful.**
- The identity is (authority `field-notes` domain, or the owning lab's scope; kind `requirement`; value `REQ-7`).
- Requirement ids are domain-owned. `runloop` may only transport them.
- The proposed `runloop:req:<uuid>` puts an engine-minted value in place of a domain identity.
- Parsing the digits of `REQ-7` into a priority field gives the id a semantic grammar the engine does not own.
- Dropping `REQ-7` is also an identity change with no explicit old-to-new relation.
- A uuid kept as a transported alias, with an explicit mapping recorded by the domain, would be lawful. Replacing the id is not.

**Fact 2: unlawful. The authority-scope coordinate is missing.**
- There are two distinct identities: (`lab-a`, `requirement`, `REQ-7`) and (`lab-b`, `requirement`, `REQ-7`).
- A flat file keyed only by `REQ-7` reduces both to a naked string, so the two live objects collide.
- The serialisation must keep the hierarchy, for example lab scope, then kind, then value. Any cross-lab aliasing must be stated explicitly.

**Fact 3: unlawful. Two things are collapsed.**
- `REQ-F2` has kind `requirement_family`: (`field-notes` domain, with the lab scope unstated, `requirement_family`, `REQ-F2`). The report treats it as a concrete `requirement`. "Satisfied" must be claimed against concrete requirement ids, or the family claim must be stated as a family claim.
- The dashboard object is named only by `2.1.0`. That is a release version, not an object identity. The object's own authority, kind (for example `carrier_surface` or a published asset) and value are missing. `2.1.0` can appear only as a publication-version coordinate on that identity. It cannot replace the identity.

**Fact 4: unlawful.**
- The retired identity (`field-notes`, `requirement`, `REQ-9`) was silently reused for a different, unrelated object.
- The old identity must not be reused for a different object.
- The new requirement needs a fresh value. Alternatively, an explicit relation between old and new must be recorded, and the old retirement would still stand.
- No relation is recorded, so traceability and supersession are broken.

## 2. Ownership of identities

| Identity | Owner | Other party may | Other party may not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Run ids (`r-8f3a2c`), kind `run` | `runloop` engine | `field-notes` may transport, store and reference them as opaque values. | `field-notes` may not parse them, assign them, or give them meaning. |
| Event ids (`e-000193`), kind `event` | `runloop` engine | Same as run ids. | Same as run ids. |
| Requirement ids (`REQ-7`), also family ids (`REQ-F2`) | `field-notes` domain. Each lab owns its own requirement scope inside the product. | `runloop` may transport and carry them. | `runloop` may not replace, regenerate, parse or invent meaning for them. |
| Acceptance criterion ids (`AC-7.1`) | `field-notes` domain | `runloop` may transport them. | `runloop` may not rewrite, parse or reinterpret them. |

The engine may use opaque, counter-generated ids for its own objects, optimised for uniqueness and replay safety. The domain owns the grammar, kinds and uniqueness rules of its own ids.
