Same-family working review. It is working evidence and not independent assessment. The interpretation and the cases in `cases.md` were not both written by me: I wrote `cases.md` before opening the program, and the program and tools were authored by another activation.

## 1. Basis and method

- Subject: `a_c/i3/` at commit `416bdb5`, read with `git show`. Source: the members at `416bdb5` (`git diff --stat 3ab7ef9 416bdb5 -- standards/*.md` shows only `standards/README.md` changed, and that file is not one of the eight encoded members).
- Order: `cases.md` was saved first (see its "Basis and order"). Then I opened `axiomatic-program.json` (by script: structure, then every clause's operator, force, standing and arguments), `tools/spec.py` (lines 1 to 40, 100 to 134, 159 to 170), `coverage.json`, `validation-report.json`, `checker-commands.log` and the eight `dispositions/*.md` files (by script). I did not read `SIGNATURE.md`, `bindings.json`, the fragments, the logical-constraint map or the projections.
- Clause URI prefix: `urn:stdo-3:claude:i3:clause:`. Only the short id is written below.
- Terms. "Structured" means the operator, force, standing and argument fields of a clause. "Statement" means the quoted source text carried in the clause. Findings concern what a consumer sees when it reads the structured fields without the statement.
- Limits. I judged the clauses that bear on the 8 cases and ran four mechanical checks over all 64 clauses. I did not compare every statement with its source byte by byte (the build tool quotes them; I spot-checked the ones in the cases). A mechanical pass is not fidelity (ORACLE C6).

## 3. Required checks

- "Not required" read as "prohibited", or a preference as an obligation. Found for "not required": `block-outside-claim` (and a softer hardening in `authority-kinds-never-collapse`). Found for "preference": none; no clause carries a preference, and the sentence that would (AXIOMATIC_CALCULUS.md:1305-1306) is not encoded. Mechanical scan of all 9 `forbids` clauses: the other seven restate source sentences that use never, none, only or neither.
- A maps-to clause that maps to more than one core value: none. A script over all 28 maps-to clauses found exactly one `core-value` argument each, all five core values valid, and an owner and predicate on every clause.
- Hollow policy-choice. Form: none; all 32 policy-choice clauses have a non-empty `choice` and `owner`. Substance: five of the AC-007 constraint maps are same-name identity maps (`ac-indeterminate` to `indeterminate`, `ac-invalid-basis` to `invalid_basis`, `ac-holds`, `ac-failed`, `ac-out-of-scope`). They are labelled policy choices with `among` "the five core values", but they leave nothing open; they restate a renaming. That is a vacuous choice, not an error.
- Dispositions. For each of the eight members (FOUNDATIONS, IDENTITY, PROFILES, ACTIVATION, CONFORMANCE, EVALUATION, AXIOMATIC_CALCULUS, REFERENCE_FRAME) every heading of the S2 member has exactly one row with exactly one allowed value: 204 headings, 204 rows, no missing, extra or duplicate row. Totals 15 `encoded`, 8 `no determination`, 181 `not-encoded-i3`, equal to the README. Every one of the 64 clauses is cited by an encoded row, and every clause cited exists. Limit of this result: a row's value applies to a whole heading section. The 15 `encoded` rows each cover only some sentences of their section (for example `#constraint-surfaces` lines 1273-1351 has 2 clauses, `#3-commitments` has seven commitment clauses and no P4a clause and none for Foundation to law). A reader of the disposition table cannot see the unencoded remainder. Under ORACLE C3 and J14 a fragment must not be offered as the whole; the README gives the coverage at section level and frame-index level only.
