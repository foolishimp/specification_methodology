# Reference Frame Method

## Canonical Compression

A human or computational actor has finite context, finite attention, finite
time, and bounded capability. A material workspace may contain more relevant
truth, history, topology, authority, and evidence than one actor can evaluate
at once.

The Reference Frame Method makes that limit explicit:

```text
declared evaluation family
  + exact workspace and topology basis
  + finite material relations and coordinates
  + applicable invariants and authorities
  + admissible evidence and exclusions
  + actor capability envelope
  = activated reference frame
  -> satisfied | falsified | indeterminate | out_of_frame | invalid_basis
```

A project engages a body of work through a declared frame set:

```text
workspace and governed outcome
  + accumulated practice and historical failures
  + available actor capabilities
  + human judgment
  -> declared frame set
  -> bounded evaluations
  -> conjunction or translation where required
  -> coverage ledger and residual uncertainty
  -> empirical frame-set revision
```

The reference frame is the atomic engagement unit. Frame-set selection,
activation, evaluation, conjunction, translation, coverage, and revision are
separate method functions. A frame does not need to contain a universal theory
of its possible relationships before it can support a bounded evaluation.

`Atomic engagement unit` names the unit that can be activated and evaluated as
one bounded context. It does not assert that every relation inside the frame is
one irreducible semantic atom. A frame may carry several semantic atoms and
several owner relations without merging their meaning or authority.

---

## Position

This method governs how finite actors construct and use bounded reasoning or
execution contexts over a changing body of work. It applies to human work,
agentic work, deterministic evaluation, probabilistic construction, assurance,
and mixed work where the governing product or method permits them.

The [foundations](FOUNDATIONS.md#bounded-construction) supply the finite-context
and finite-attention premises. Given a task, relevant dependencies and a capable
actor, they require a sufficient bounded acquisition rather than an assumed
global context. RF-002 through RF-004 refine the core acquisition contract for
frame activation; RF-013 and RF-014
decide reconstructability and fit. Choosing a particular frame partition,
evaluation protocol or actor arrangement remains a declared method choice.

This member owns frame declaration, activation, evaluation, coverage and
revision as foundations-tier frame law. Its authority vocabulary remains scoped
to those relations; other operations retain their owning contracts' grants.
It consumes the shared [result algebra](FOUNDATIONS.md#result-algebra),
[conjunction](FOUNDATIONS.md#result-conjunction) and
[workspace/context relation](FOUNDATIONS.md#workspace-and-context) at the
deciding operations below. Its finer outcomes refine that algebra; they do not
create another core result family. The
[transformation library](TRANSFORMATION_LIBRARY.md) binds acquisition,
evaluation and result consumption without imposing an execution sequence.

This is an engagement meta-method. It does not define an application profile,
role mapping, Product-owned frame-basis binding, domain Product, specification
taxonomy, design ontology, runtime, workflow, adoption relation, or assurance
implementation. External methods and Products may import or specialize these
relations under their own authority. That external relation does not enter or
amend the principles.

`REFERENCE_FRAME_METHOD.md` owns the reference-frame-evaluation bounded context
`urn:stdo:bounded-context:reference-frame-evaluation` under its exact
publication basis. This standard owns only its frame declaration, activation,
evaluation, basis, relation, result, coverage, revision, and qualification
concepts. A same-spelled profile, Product, role, or runtime carrier is not
imported by proximity or nominal match.

The method is substrate-neutral. A reference frame may be represented in prose,
structured data, a prompt, a design asset, a typed carrier, a runtime context,
or another form selected by its consumer. No class, aggregate, actor,
controller, service, registry, graph, or frame engine is required.

## Core Claims

1. No finite actor is presumed to hold the complete material workspace in
   active attention.
2. A useful frame is finite and sufficient for a declared evaluation family.
3. The evaluations to be made determine the material attention surface.
4. Human authority may declare a practical project frame set from accumulated
   practice without deriving a unique frame partition mechanically.
5. Frames may overlap, compose, refine, restrict, translate, or succeed one
   another. Hierarchy is one optional relation.
6. Several frames may be used together without composing their internal
   coordinate systems.
7. Coverage is relative to a declared workspace, governed outcome, evaluation
   inventory, basis, and time.
8. Frame selection and result use apply the shared
   [authority contract](FOUNDATIONS.md#judgment-and-authority) through RF-006.
9. Unknown and out-of-frame results are lawful pressure for reconfiguration.
   They are not failed evaluations or implied acceptance.
10. Historical failures, resets, and invisible violations are evidence for
    improving a frame declaration, activation, actor assignment, or frame set.

## Definitions

### Evaluation

An **evaluation** is a declared question whose predicate, admissible evidence,
decision authority, and operation authority where it performs a material action
are known. Its results use the [core result algebra](FOUNDATIONS.md#result-algebra)
directly or a declared reason-sensitive refinement under RF-012. An evaluation
may ask for a judgment, prediction, construction, comparison, proof, refusal,
classification, or action recommendation.

An evaluation family groups questions that require the same material relation
manifold, authority basis, and result binding or refinement of that one algebra.
Similar wording does not prove that two evaluations belong to the same family.

### Workspace

A **workspace** is the bounded body of material being reasoned over or acted
upon. It may include constitutional sources, domain assets, designs,
implementations, tools, runtime state, evidence, historical records, external
dependencies, and human decisions.

The workspace is broader than a repository. A repository is one possible
carrier. [Workspace and context](FOUNDATIONS.md#workspace-and-context) governs
the distinction between this material state and an actor's acquired view.
Observed state can defeat a stale summary; the summary cannot change the state.
External effects require observations from their owning surfaces.

### Basis

A **basis** identifies the exact state against which an evaluation is made. It
includes every version, identity, time, topology, authority, configuration, or
evidence coordinate whose change can alter the evaluation.

A basis may be immutable or moving. A moving basis must declare how advancement
is detected and when reevaluation is required.

### Material relation

A relation is **material** to an evaluation when a lawful change to that
relation can change the evaluation result, invalidate its basis, change the
admissible evidence, change who may perform a material operation, or change who
may decide it.

Materiality is evaluation-relative. A relation may be material in one frame and
irrelevant in another.

### Material-relation manifold

The **material-relation manifold** is the finite, jointly interpretable set of
variables, entities, identities, states, relations, invariants, authorities,
observations, and exclusions needed for an evaluation family.

`Manifold` names a bounded relation space with potentially overlapping
coordinate charts. It does not require geometric mathematics or a runtime
manifold abstraction.

### Coordinate

A **coordinate** is the complete local position from which a value, event,
decision, observation, or action is interpreted. It includes the basis,
identity, lineage, input, evidence, and authority information required by the
evaluation.

Equal names, values, shapes, locations, or apparent topology positions do not
establish coordinate equality. Equality follows a declared relation.

### Topology

**Topology** is the relation structure among the material entities,
capabilities, authorities, lifecycle state spaces, adjacencies, and seams
visible to a frame at one basis. The frame need not contain the workspace's
complete topology.

A state-value change preserves the frame topology only when it preserves every
material entity, relation, lifecycle, authority, capability, adjacency, and
seam. A state transition that changes any such relation is a topology mutation
and may invalidate the frame's coordinates.

### Evidence

**Evidence** is the exact observation or artifact admitted for an evaluation.
Evidence carries provenance, subject identity, basis, and scope. Evidence does
not become semantic truth or decision authority merely because it is available
inside a frame.

For reliance on that evidence, bind
[Construction assurance](FOUNDATIONS.md#construction-assurance) to the frame's
predicate, observations and consuming decision. Assertion provenance justifies
the expected predicate; execution provenance identifies the acquired subject
and result. A sufficiently small frame or an authenticated observation alone
does not establish a sound, complete decision on its predicate.

### Authority

The method distinguishes:

- **frame-set authority**: the human authority that declares or accepts the
  project frame set;
- **semantic authority**: the owner of meaning for a material relation;
- **evaluation authority**: the role permitted to perform an evaluation;
- **operation authority**: the owner-issued grant to construct, transform,
  admit, publish, or mutate a material subject where an evaluation performs
  such an operation;
- **decision authority**: the role permitted to accept, reject, or otherwise
  dispose its result; and
- **actor identity**: the human, agent, tool, or process performing work.

These are the frame vocabulary used by
[Judgment and authority](FOUNDATIONS.md#judgment-and-authority); apply that
owner's separation and conservation to declaration, selection, activation and
execution. A read-only evaluation declares no operation authority. An effectful
evaluation names the existing owner-issued operation grant it applies.

Human authority may act directly or through an explicitly admitted bounded
proxy where the governing identity and authority law permits it. Proxy identity
and human-authority identity remain distinct.

### Disjointness And Independence

Three relations remain distinct:

- **frame disjointness** is structural: two frames share no material variable,
  coordinate, evidence, or result dependency for the declared evaluations;
- **evaluation independence** is procedural: separately activated evaluations
  do not consume or influence one another's unclosed working results, although
  they may intentionally inspect the same subject, overlap in their manifolds,
  and later supply closed results to a conjunction; and
- **assurance independence** is claim-relative: the governing assurance law
  states the required non-authorship, evidence-acquisition, reference-exposure,
  actor, and configuration separation for a verdict to count as independent
  assurance.

Disjoint frames need not be independently evaluated, and independently
activated evaluations need not be disjoint. The unqualified word
`independent` does not establish any of these relations by itself.

### Capability envelope

A **capability envelope** declares the competence, context capacity, tools,
access, applicable evaluation- or assurance-independence conditions, and
configuration required to activate a frame and perform its evaluations.

The same frame may be usable by one actor and unusable by another. Actor
capability may justify a different frame size or activation sequence. It cannot
waive an invariant or widen authority.

### Reference frame

A **reference frame** is a finite specification of the attention, coordinates,
authority, evidence, and result relations required to perform one declared
evaluation family over a stated basis.

Formally, a frame may be described as:

\[
F = \langle Q, B, M, C, I, A, E, X, R, J, K, D \rangle
\]

where:

- \(Q\) is the evaluation family;
- \(B\) is the workspace and topology basis;
- \(M\) is the material-relation manifold;
- \(C\) is the coordinate and equality law;
- \(I\) is the applicable invariant set;
- \(A\) is the authority relation;
- \(E\) is the admissible evidence and provenance law;
- \(X\) is the exclusion and prohibited-assumption set;
- \(R\) is the shared core result algebra with the frame's declared refinements;
- \(J\) is the invalidation and rechart condition;
- \(K\) is the actor capability envelope; and
- \(D\) is the optional set of declared dependencies, overlaps, inputs, and
  outputs involving other frames.

This tuple specifies the relation. It is not a required schema or runtime
carrier.

Operationally, a reference frame is an evaluation contract that defines the
finite attention scope required for one evaluation family. A prompt, context
packet, symbolic program, or other representation is a projection of an
activated frame, not the frame itself. A frame is bound when every material
coordinate above resolves to an exact source, subject, basis, authority,
evidence route, actor envelope, result relation, and invalidation condition.
The binding may be expressed entirely in source-linked prose; representation
cannot create, widen, or replace it.

### Activated frame

An **activated frame** binds one frame declaration to an exact evaluation,
workspace subject, basis, actor, and evidence acquisition boundary. A reusable
frame declaration is not evidence that a particular activation was correct.

### Frame set

A **frame set** is the human-declared family of frames available for a governed
outcome or workspace. It may intentionally contain overlaps, alternatives,
specialist frames, broad exploratory frames, and separately activated assurance
frames.

A frame set is not required to be a partition, tree, stack, or exhaustive
ontology.

### Active frame configuration

An **active frame configuration** is the set of frame activations and declared
result relations used for one evaluation or decision. It may contain one frame
or several disjoint, overlapping, or composed frames. Separately activated
evaluations may be disjoint or overlapping.

### Lens

A **lens** is a reusable family of questions applied across a frame
configuration. Authority, capability and reuse, temporal behavior, transport,
and proof are examples of lenses when named abstractly.

A lens becomes a frame only when it is instantiated with a bounded evaluation,
basis, material manifold, evidence, authorities, result algebra, and capability
envelope.

### Role, persona, abstraction, and component

A **role** allocates responsibility or authority. A **persona** alters an
actor's approach or salience. An **abstraction** hides or contracts detail. A
**component** is a structural part of a subject.

Each may help construct or activate a frame. None is a reference frame by
identity alone. One component may appear in several frames, and one frame may
cross several components.

### RF-001 Evaluation Relativity

Every frame declares the evaluation family it supports. A frame with no
declared evaluation family has no testable sufficiency boundary.

An activation uses that declaration to establish that its actual question is
in the family. Membership of the frame in an available frame set, or a similar
task name, does not establish that relation. A question outside the declared
family needs a suitable frame or the applicable scoped result; renaming the
question does not preserve the original obligation.

### RF-002 Finite Attention

An activated frame contains a finite attention surface that fits the selected
actor's declared capability envelope. A frame that requires undeclared ambient
memory, conversation history, or an effectively global workspace context is
not reconstructable for that actor.

### RF-003 Material Sufficiency

Apply [Contract application: Support](TRANSFORMATION_LIBRARY.md#contract-application)
to the evaluation family `Q`: its known material relations, as defined by
[Material relation](#material-relation), enter `M` or a declared authoritative
input. An omitted material relation makes the activation insufficient. This
known-relation boundary does not require proving that no undiscovered relation
exists.

### RF-004 Irrelevance Exclusion

Apply [Workspace and context](FOUNDATIONS.md#workspace-and-context) to `Q`'s
attention surface: exclude relations established irrelevant to that evaluation,
retaining any material needed to establish irrelevance. A frame does not expand
merely because adjacent workspace material exists.

### RF-005 Exact Basis And Coordinates

Every result binds its exact subject, basis, coordinates, and evidence scope.
Apply [Change impact and evidence retention](FOUNDATIONS.md#change-impact-and-evidence-retention)
to proposed reuse, with the frame's declared current-basis requirements and
coordinate equality. An attempted evaluation whose stale, ambiguous or mismatched
governing basis cannot support interpretation of its bound predicate returns
`invalid_basis` or requires reactivation on a resolved basis. Stale supporting
evidence under an interpretable governing basis instead follows
[RF-012](#rf-012-closed-results): insufficient support leaves the predicate
`indeterminate` unless other admissible evidence decides it. A basis advance is assessed against those
requirements; it does not by itself invalidate a historical result. The original
record stays bound to its original subject and basis, and reliance for a new
claim needs the owner's established applicability relation. Apparent equality
does not substitute for declared coordinate equality.

### RF-006 Authority Conservation

Apply [Judgment and authority](FOUNDATIONS.md#judgment-and-authority) to the
frame's [authority relations](#authority). Bind each evaluation, operation and
disposition to its existing owner-issued scope and mandate. Frame selection,
actor assignment, available capability, evidence and implementation reachability
are inputs to that application, not additional grant mechanisms.

### RF-007 Semantic, Evidence, And Verdict Separation

Apply [Judgment and authority](FOUNDATIONS.md#judgment-and-authority) to the
frame declaration's semantic owner, observations, evaluator, any operation grant
and disposition mandate. The declaration binds those distinct relations even
when one actor lawfully occupies several. A disposition consumes the closed
evaluation through [Dispose](TRANSFORMATION_LIBRARY.md#dispose), with its domain
conditions supplied by the owning decision contract.

### RF-008 Overlap Without Identity Collapse

Frames may share entities, variables, evidence, or coordinates. Shared material
does not establish identical meaning, basis, authority, or result. Every
cross-frame equality or translation used by an evaluation is declared where it
is needed.

### RF-009 No Assumed Hierarchy

The [configuration relation](FOUNDATIONS.md#configuration) governs allocation
of tasks to bounded frames. Roles label selected responsibilities; they are not
an exhaustive primitive inventory or a prerequisite for every task.

Frames may relate through disjointness, overlap, refinement, restriction,
translation, temporal succession, composition, or governance. Parent and child
relations apply only where explicitly declared.

Functional decomposition, authority precedence, frame configuration, subject
topology, and evaluation dependency are distinct relation structures. A
consumer may align them where the governed subject warrants it. It may not
silently treat them as one hierarchy.

### RF-010 Composition On Demand

Using several frames does not require composing their internal manifolds.
Composition law is required only when the governing evaluation combines their
internal variables, transports evidence or values across them, or treats their
operations as one material execution relation.

Independently activated evaluations may instead return closed results to one
declared conjunction or decision rule.

Use [Result conjunction](FOUNDATIONS.md#result-conjunction) for the result
relation. When the evaluation transports evidence or coordinates, or combines
operations into one execution, apply
[Composition law](TRANSFORMATION_LIBRARY.md#composition-law) to that material
join as well. The latter obligation comes from the relation the consumer
actually uses, not from the mere count of contributing frames.

### RF-011 Topology Mutation And Recharting

An activated frame declares the topology changes capable of invalidating its
coordinates or materiality judgment. A detected material topology mutation
causes reevaluation, frame revision, or recharting before the previous result
is reused.

### RF-012 Closed Results

Every evaluation returns the [core result](FOUNDATIONS.md#result-algebra)
bound to its exact frame, activation, subject, basis and predicate. The five
verdicts and their conjunction have that single owner. A frame-specific outcome
retains its original domain meaning and has one explicit mapping to the core
verdict for the declared predicate. An outcome lacking its required reason,
outside the declared mapping, or without its mapping's required support follows
the core missing-reason rule; its label supplies no deciding evidence.

An activation attempt and the work it would authorize are different subjects.
A refused activation records its own result on “this frame may be activated
for this actor, subject and basis”, with its cause and evidence. Established
failure of an admission condition maps to `falsified`; unresolved admissibility
maps to `indeterminate`. Capability, authority, basis and topology are the
conditions being checked, not the admission check's own capability or basis.
A declared ambiguity that prevents admission therefore falsifies admission;
uncertainty about whether that ambiguity exists remains indeterminate. If the
admission check itself lacks scope or a usable basis, its result follows the
core diagnosis rule instead. Missing causes follow the core missing-reason rule.

Where the plan requires the downstream work predicate, a separately established
capability or basis diagnosis can report `out_of_frame` or `invalid_basis` on
that predicate with its actual performance. Otherwise its slot stays absent
with the observed performance fact. Neither case fabricates a Worker return,
and an admission prohibition alone is no counterexample to downstream fitness.
Successful
activation satisfies admission only. The work remains `not_performed` until
actually undertaken. Each result retains performance and known effect state.

Where execution cannot be confirmed, retain the core `unconfirmed` performance
state. An absent return is not evidence that an invoked operation did not run.

A domain may refine these cases but cannot erase a failure, unknown, refusal,
non-performance or uncertain effect, or promote candidate availability into
fitness, acceptance or authority.

### RF-013 Reconstructable Activation

A fresh competent actor inside the declared capability envelope can reconstruct
the activated evaluation from the frame declaration, exact subject, basis, and
admissible inputs without undocumented conversational or historical context.

### RF-014 Capability Fit

Frame adequacy includes actor usability. When the material manifold exceeds an
actor's capability envelope, human authority may select another actor, refine
the frame, sequence activations, or provide a lawful compression. The actor may
not silently omit material relations to fit context.

### RF-015 Residual Uncertainty

Coverage and evaluation results retain known uncertainty. Unresolved relations,
uncovered evaluations, contested evidence, and unknown capability remain
explicit. Absence of a recorded counterexample is not evidence of universal
completeness.

### RF-016 Implementation Neutrality

This method requires the declared relations and observable outcomes. It does
not require a frame runtime, universal carrier, registry, controller, prompt
format, repository layout, collaboration topology, worker count, or software
framework.

### RF-017 Proportional Compression

A frame declaration compresses accumulated practice lawfully only when it
preserves the material distinctions and failure detections required by its
whole declared evaluation family, with adequate capability fit under RF-014.
A frame view applies [Faithful projection](TRANSFORMATION_LIBRARY.md#faithful-projection)
from the owning declaration and material subject to the actor's evaluation
`Q`. Its required distinctions include `Q`'s failure detections; its adequacy
includes the capability fit in RF-014.

Compression does not require a unique smallest frame. Intentional overlap is
retained where it supplies independently activated evaluation, different
capability fit, different evidence altitude, or useful fault detection. Frames
are merged only when the merged frame preserves evaluation meaning, authority,
capability fit, and proportional reasoning cost.

### Engagement Atom And Semantic Prime

A reference frame is atomic at engagement altitude: it is activated and
evaluated as one bounded attention unit. Semantic irreducibility is a separate
question. One frame may contain several semantic atoms, and one semantic atom
may participate in several frames.

Where an adopting method supplies a Prime or equivalent semantic-contraction
law, that law governs the domain atom family. Frame-set contraction then asks a
different question: whether two frame declarations support the same evaluation
relation without material difference in evidence, authority, capability fit,
evaluation or assurance independence, or failure detection. A frame set is not
contracted into a disjoint minimum merely because its material variables
overlap.

## Human Frame-Set Declaration

Human authority may declare an initial project frame set from accumulated
development practice, known failure modes, domain expertise, Product risk, and
knowledge of available agents or other actors.

The declaration need not reconstruct the frame set from the workspace bottom
up. It records:

- the governed workspace and outcome;
- the known evaluation inventory;
- the selected frames and their capability envelopes;
- intentional overlaps and separately activated checks;
- known frame dependencies or translations;
- semantic, evaluation, operation where material, decision, and acceptance
  authority;
- known exclusions and residual uncertainty; and
- conditions for revision.

Human selection is a lawful source of method structure. It does not prove the
selected set complete and does not grant the frames authority over the subject
relations they inspect.

### Frame-Set Optimization

Frame-set selection is a constrained coverage problem. Let \(E(W,G)\) be the
known material evaluations for workspace \(W\) and governed outcome \(G\). Let
\(S\) be the selected frame set.

The baseline coverage constraint is:

\[
\forall e \in E(W,G),\ \exists F \in S,\ \exists a:\
a \models K_F \land capable(F,a,e)
\]

where \(K_F\) is the frame's capability envelope and \(a\) is an available
actor inside it.

Selection also accounts for:

- actor context and competence;
- evaluation risk and consequence;
- acquisition and reasoning cost;
- evidence availability;
- useful independently activated overlap;
- translation and reconciliation cost;
- historical blind spots; and
- reversibility when the frame set proves inadequate.

The method does not prescribe an optimization algorithm. Human authority may
accept a non-minimal frame set where its additional cost buys material risk
reduction or better actor fit.

These selection considerations create no universal resource ledger, benchmark,
budget or measurement gate. A resource control applies only through an
explicitly selected frame and its owning applicability and work scope. Frame
selection cannot waive another applicable semantic, authority, evidence or
independence constraint.

## Method Functions

The reference frame atom and the functions operating over it remain distinct.
An application may implement the following relations in any conforming form:

```text
declare(human_authority, workspace, outcome, practices, capabilities)
  -> frame_set

activate(frame, evaluation, subject, basis, actor)
  -> activated_frame | activation_refusal

evaluate(activated_frame, admissible_evidence)
  -> result + evidence + residuals

conjoin(decision_rule, frame_results)
  -> core_result + retained_conflicts + residuals

translate(source_frame, target_frame, value_or_evidence, mapping)
  -> translated_value | declared_loss | refusal

audit_coverage(workspace, outcome, evaluation_inventory, frame_set)
  -> coverage_ledger

revise(frame_set, new_evidence, failures, capability_change, topology_change)
  -> proposed_frame_set_delta
```

These signatures describe method relations. They do not require callable
software interfaces. Their returned values, refusal words and decision
outcomes are domain outputs; each evaluated claim about them uses RF-012's
shared core result on its exact predicate. A governing decision rule may consume
these results through the library's [Dispose](TRANSFORMATION_LIBRARY.md#dispose)
responsibility under its bound authority; that decision is separate from the
conjunction verdict. A returned `decision` does not prove it is authorised or
correct without that judgment. The frame binds any
finer case mapping before its outcome is consumed.

## Engagement

### Establish The Evaluation Basis

Bind the [reference-frame tuple](#reference-frame) to the governed outcome or
decision and actual workspace subject: `Q` selects the evaluation, `B` and `C`
its basis and coordinates, `A` the applicable authorities, `K` the available
actor envelope, and `J` the stale or out-of-scope conditions. The declaration
and material inputs supply the remaining tuple coordinates.

### Applicability And Required Results

The owning evaluation and scope declarations determine which frame obligations
apply to the exact subject. Evaluate their declared predicates from sufficient
basis-bound facts. A true predicate requires the applicable evaluation and
result; a false predicate excludes only that obligation under its cited
authority. Missing or ambiguous premises leave applicability unresolved, not
false. A frame name, available tool or listed family is no activation trigger
by itself. Where applicability needs residual semantic judgment, retain that
judgment and its evidence rather than representing it as a computed fact.

Use that applicability determination to bind the required set in
[Result conjunction](#result-conjunction), including unresolved applicability.
Other applicable constraints remain in force even if only one evaluation detects
them. Qualification distinguishes true, false and unknown premises, missing or
wrong-subject required results, and a sufficient positive result within a finite
declared fixture; always skipping or always activating cannot meet that contract.

### Select And Activate

The actor or assigning authority selects one frame or declared configuration.
Apply [Contract application](TRANSFORMATION_LIBRARY.md#contract-application)
to “this frame may be activated for evaluation `q`, subject `s`, basis `B` and
actor `a`”. The tuple binds the contract: `M`, `I` and `D` supply material
support and preservation; `C` supplies equality; `A` supplies actual grants;
`E` supplies admissible evidence; `J` supplies invalidators; and `K` supplies
capability. RF-002 through RF-004 and RF-013/014 set frame sufficiency and fit.
RF-001 binds `q` to the declared family `Q`; the same binding determines which
relations are material under RF-003. An available general-purpose frame is
therefore insufficient evidence of fit for a particular question. Inspect its
actual family, material inputs and actor conditions under the shared contract.
The acquired packet applies RF-017 to that declaration and current material
subject. Prior results and uncertain effects use the shared contract's
currentness rule.

When no available frame and actor pairing can lawfully perform the evaluation,
activation returns an RF-012 activation refusal. A separately established
`out_of_frame` result on the downstream work predicate follows RF-012's rule;
it is not fabricated from the activation refusal. Work does not proceed through
implied context.

### Evaluate

The evaluator applies the frame's declared invariants, evidence rules, and
predicate-bound core result refinement under RF-012. It distinguishes semantic law, observed evidence, inference,
and decision. It records every assumption whose failure could change the
result.

### Return A Closed Result

Return RF-012's core record with frame, activation, actor and `J` invalidation
coordinates. Apply [Faithful projection](TRANSFORMATION_LIBRARY.md#faithful-projection)
to its handoff: the consuming decision must recover the material result and
provenance without private working context. Result reuse applies
[Change impact and evidence retention](FOUNDATIONS.md#change-impact-and-evidence-retention)
and retains revised results' predecessor/successor relation. Apply
[Independent assessment](FOUNDATIONS.md#independent-assessment) to required
independent frame evaluations: they may share valid mechanical evidence while
each assessor supplies its own sufficiently acquired evidence and judgment under
the declared conditions.

### Reconfigure When Necessary

An `out_of_frame`, invalid-basis, repeated indeterminate, or incompatible result
causes one of:

- supply missing admissible evidence;
- select another actor inside the frame's capability envelope;
- activate another existing frame;
- refine or extend a frame declaration;
- revise the frame set;
- change the evaluation; or
- re-enter the governing Product or method relation.

The disposition follows the cause. A difficult evaluation alone does not prove
that another frame is required.

## Using Frames In Conjunction

Frames participate together through four distinct relations.

### Independently Activated Co-Evaluation

Several frames may evaluate the same subject through separate activations and
return closed results. They may overlap in subject, manifold, or evidence
sources, but neither evaluation consumes or influences the other's unclosed
working result. This is useful for assurance independence, different evidence
altitudes, or distinct failure perspectives.

For a mutual-assurance claim, apply
[Parallel assurance and provenance](FOUNDATIONS.md#parallel-assurance-and-provenance)
to the selected paths and their material shared causes. Separate activations
satisfying the declared authorship conditions do not by themselves establish
statistical independence or validate a shared governing expectation.

### Result Conjunction

A declared decision rule consumes closed frame results through
[core conjunction](FOUNDATIONS.md#result-conjunction). Its frame-specific
binding states:

- which results are mandatory;
- how conflicting or indeterminate contributions are routed for re-evaluation;
- which basis relations must agree;
- what decision authority owns the conjunction; and
- whether any result may veto or defer the decision.

These parameters bind the mandatory claims and material joins before the core
rule is applied. Its result carries coverage, conflicts and residuals; the
decision rule cannot replace a required constraint with a count of agreeing
frames or acquire a contributing frame's semantic authority.

### Evidence Or Coordinate Translation

Apply [Faithful projection](TRANSFORMATION_LIBRARY.md#faithful-projection)
with the producing frame's value or evidence as source and the receiving
frame's evaluation as consumer. Bind both `C` coordinate/equality laws and the
translation's refusal conditions alongside the shared preservation, loss and
provenance requirements.

The returned `translated_value`, `declared_loss` or `refusal` is retained with
its cause. Evaluate “this translation meets the bound target-use contract on
these source/target coordinates”: supported preservation, including expressly
admitted loss, maps to `satisfied`; an established forbidden loss, equality,
identity, basis-compatibility or authority violation maps to `falsified`;
undecided preservation or loss permission maps to `indeterminate`. If that
evaluation itself lacks material scope/capability or a usable basis, the map is
`out_of_frame` or `invalid_basis`, respectively. Missing causes use RF-012's
missing-reason rule. A coordinate mismatch established by a valid check is a
failed translation condition, not necessarily an invalid basis for the check.
All cases preserve actual performance and effects; refusal does not establish
the target predicate the untranslated evidence was intended to support.

### Material Composition

Apply [Composition law](TRANSFORMATION_LIBRARY.md#composition-law) to the
frame-governed operations and the declared outer execution or reasoning
predicate. The application binds shared invariants, material effect or decision
order, closure and failure conditions, using each frame's `A`, `C` and `D`
relations for authority, coordinates and joins.

Composition is introduced only when the evaluation requires it. A possible
future interaction does not require a general composition law in every frame.

## Frame Relations

Frames may relate as:

- **disjoint**: no material variable, coordinate, evidence, or result dependency
  is shared for the declared evaluations;
- **overlapping**: some material variables, evidence, or entities are shared;
- **complementary**: distinct evaluations jointly inform one decision;
- **alternative**: different frames can evaluate the same claim under declared
  capability or evidence conditions;
- **refinement**: one frame resolves a material distinction left aggregated in
  another;
- **restriction**: one frame evaluates a lawful subset of another frame's
  domain;
- **translated**: values or evidence move through a declared coordinate map;
- **temporally successive**: a later frame evaluates a changed basis;
- **composed**: several frame relations form one governed evaluation or action;
  or
- **governing and subordinate**: one frame inherits declared constraints from
  another where a real authority or decomposition relation exists.

No one relation is the default universal frame topology. A project declares
only the relations needed for its evaluations and coverage claims.

## Workspace Coverage

F1 requires coverage relative to a governed outcome; F2 and F3 require evidence
on its exact current basis; F4 makes interactions material; F5 rules out
inferring unknown completeness from a finite inventory; F6 reserves exclusion
and disposition to their owners. Consequently a coverage claim needs supported
evaluation, interaction and authority coverage plus its residual boundary.
This method chooses the ledger vocabulary and eight criteria below. Human
frame-set authority selects the inventory and required independence under the
governing outcome; no foundation determines one universal inventory.

Apply [Applicability And Required Results](#applicability-and-required-results)
to the inventory before consuming its results. Apply Foundations conjunction
to the resulting required predicates and material interactions. This separates
three claims: the inventory covers the selected scope, every applicable slot
has admissible support, and the supported results establish the governed
predicate. A populated ledger or a satisfied result in every received slot
alone establishes neither of the other claims.

### Coverage Basis

Coverage is evaluated against a declared tuple:

\[
Coverage(W, G, B, E, S, T)
\]

where:

- \(W\) is the workspace;
- \(G\) is the governed outcome;
- \(B\) is the exact basis;
- \(E\) is the known evaluation inventory;
- \(S\) is the declared frame set; and
- \(T\) is the time or immutable checkpoint of the claim.

Changing any material member may invalidate the coverage result.

### Evaluation Inventory

The coverage evaluator derives the known evaluation inventory from every
material available source, including where applicable:

- goals, intent, Product, requirements, and accepted method;
- accepted design, Ontology, identities, lifecycles, authorities, and
  algorithms;
- implementation capabilities and externally observable behavior;
- operational, release, security, safety, and governance obligations;
- direct and indirect dependencies and interaction seams;
- evidence, tests, scenarios, qualification, and acceptance claims;
- historical tickets, check-ins, commentary, postmortems, incidents, and
  rejected candidates;
- days-long loops, resets, repeated repairs, and discovered rival paths;
- known agent capability limits and prior invisible violations; and
- applicable external law, contracts, or standards.

The inventory records provenance and materiality. Artifact presence, file
count, operation count, test count, or repository reachability does not prove
evaluation coverage.

### Coverage Dimensions

The audit applies cross-cutting lenses to the evaluation inventory. Typical
dimensions include:

- outcome and capability;
- identity and authority;
- lifecycle and temporal behavior;
- topology and interaction seams;
- transport and representation;
- evidence and proof;
- change, failure, recovery, and rollback;
- historical regression; and
- actor capability and context fit.

These are coverage lenses. They become frames only when separately instantiated
as bounded evaluation families.

### Coverage Ledger

The coverage ledger contains at least:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| evaluation | exact question or predicate |
| provenance | source establishing materiality |
| subject and basis | exact workspace coordinate |
| selected frames | frames capable of evaluating it |
| actor envelope | competence and context required |
| semantic authority | owner of evaluated meaning |
| operation authority | existing owner grant for construction, transformation, admission, publication, or mutation where the evaluation performs it; otherwise none |
| decision authority | owner of disposition |
| evidence | required observations and provenance |
| dependencies | material frame or seam relations |
| status | recorded coverage state used by the coverage predicate below |
| residual | uncertainty, exclusion, or re-entry condition |

The baseline coverage states describe the allocation/evidence record; they do
not report whether the row's underlying evaluation predicate is true:

| Recorded state | Input meaning |
|---|---|
| `covered` | a capable frame is identified with its coverage support |
| `separately_covered` | such coverage is separately activated, with the required independence support |
| `conditionally_covered` | coverage depends on named conditions whose support is recorded |
| `conflicted` | named coverage or frame-result contributions conflict |
| `uncovered` | a known evaluation lacks the required coverage |
| `unknown` | evidence does not establish its coverage state |
| `excluded_by_authority` | a cited owner decision excludes the evaluation within its exact scope |

The [Operational Full Coverage](#operational-full-coverage) predicate evaluates
these inputs. No state is a fixed verdict: `covered` can conceal unsupported
capability, and an exclusion can be lawful, prohibited or unresolved. An
exclusion decision's own authority result remains separately predicate-bound
under the core decision rule; recording the state supplies no grant.

### Interaction Coverage

Evaluation-row coverage is insufficient when two covered relations interact in
a way capable of changing the governed outcome. Every material interaction is
covered by one frame, a declared conjunction, a translation, or a material
composition evaluation.

### Authority Coverage

Every material evaluation identifies semantic and decision authority. Every
material operation also identifies its existing construction, transformation,
admission, publication, or mutation authority. A frame set with broad
observational reach, an unresolved decision owner, or an effectful path without
operation authority is not closed.

### Failure Coverage

Coverage includes known success, refusal, boundary, integration, recovery,
reconstruction, and competing-path cases where they are material. Historical
failures remain first-class coverage inputs until a declared frame detects or
excludes their recurrence.

### High-Risk Separately Activated Coverage

Human authority may require independently activated frame coverage for
evaluations whose failure has high consequence, weak observability, strong
confirmation bias, or a history of local proof masking. Such coverage uses a
separately activated evaluation relation and declares any additional
assurance-independence conditions. A persona label alone establishes neither
relation.

### Operational Full Coverage

A frame set has **operational full coverage** for its declared tuple when:

1. every known material evaluation is covered by at least one capable frame;
2. every material interaction has a declared evaluation, conjunction,
   translation, or composition relation;
3. every evaluation has semantic and decision authority and every material
   operation has its inherited operation authority;
4. every mandatory claim has an admissible evidence and refusal path;
5. required independently activated overlaps and assurance-independence
   conditions are present;
6. no unresolved frame-result conflict is hidden by aggregation;
7. excluded evaluations cite governing authority; and
8. uncovered and unknown residuals are explicit and compatible with the claim.

The exact predicate is “the declared frame set has operational full coverage
for `Coverage(W, G, B, E, S, T)` under these eight criteria”. Evaluate each
applicable criterion and required seam through RF-012, then use
[core conjunction](FOUNDATIONS.md#result-conjunction). Supported satisfaction
of every required criterion maps to `satisfied`; a demonstrated uncovered
mandatory evaluation, unlawful exclusion or other defeated criterion maps to
`falsified`; undecided truth of a required criterion, including whether a
condition, conflict or residual is compatible with the claim, maps to
`indeterminate` unless the core precedence preserves an established failure or
diagnosis. An unusable evaluation basis maps to `invalid_basis`; material scope
or capability outside the coverage evaluator's frame maps to `out_of_frame`.
Missing causes or unsupported state labels use the core missing-reason rule.
Retain the ledger, leaves, actual performance and effects. A successful audit
can thus establish a falsified coverage claim; an absent audit remains absent.

Operational full coverage is relative to its tuple. It is not a claim that no
undiscovered evaluation exists.

## Completeness Boundary

Without an independently established closed-domain completeness predicate, a
finite frame set cannot establish the absence of every undiscovered material
evaluation solely by inspecting its own declared inventory. A finite formally
closed domain may support exhaustive coverage relative to that domain and its
completeness predicate. The result does not generalize beyond that boundary.

Formal incompleteness results motivate the broader epistemic caution; they do
not directly prove that a particular workspace or frame set is incomplete.

Coverage therefore remains falsifiable and externally challengeable. Challenge
sources include:

- human knowledge outside the current frame set;
- independent review;
- historical failures and predecessor systems;
- mutation, adversarial, and counterexample search;
- live operation and user behavior;
- adjacent workspace or domain comparison; and
- changes in actor capability, law, dependencies, or topology.

The method requires an explicit residual boundary. It does not require a claim
of metaphysical or universal completeness.

## Discovery And Revision

### Missing-Frame Signals

The following observations trigger frame diagnosis:

- a competent actor repeatedly cannot see a known material violation;
- a local repair creates or duplicates another authority path;
- work repeatedly resets after apparently valid local completion;
- long implementation loops produce no stable closure;
- proof passes while the claimed outcome remains unbound;
- a material failure is visible only after broad integration;
- reviewers repeatedly expand context without reaching a decidable question;
- two actors use the same frame name but evaluate different material relations;
  or
- a topology mutation leaves prior verdicts apparently current.

### Diagnostic Order

Diagnosis asks, in order:

1. Was the correct declared frame activated?
2. Was the subject and basis exact?
3. Was the actor inside the capability envelope?
4. Was admissible evidence available?
5. Is a material variable or invariant missing from the frame?
6. Is a required conjunction or translation missing?
7. Is the evaluation family itself wrong or incomplete?
8. Is another frame required?

This order prevents every difficult evaluation from creating another frame.

### Revision Actions

A revision may:

- clarify activation or evidence acquisition;
- change the capability envelope;
- add or remove a material relation;
- split an overloaded frame;
- merge duplicative frames where capability and authority remain sound;
- add intentional overlap;
- add or revise a conjunction or translation;
- retire a frame whose evaluation family is no longer material; or
- revise the project evaluation inventory.

Every material revision changes frame identity or revision coordinates and
states which prior activations, results, and coverage claims require
reevaluation.

## Agentic Use

An agent receives one activated frame or a declared active frame configuration,
not an implied demand to reconstruct the complete workspace from ambient
context.

The packet is the RF-017 projection of the bound frame tuple, its identity and
revision, actual subject, admissible inputs/tools, and stop/reconfiguration
conditions. [Select And Activate](#select-and-activate) decides sufficiency;
the binding below exposes these coordinates in a carrier-neutral form.

The agent may own internal reasoning and search where the governing method
permits it. It returns through the declared result relation. Hidden internal
reasoning is not a substitute for required evidence, provenance, or authority.

Frame size is optimized for reliable evaluation, not maximum context loading.
A more capable agent may activate a broader frame. A less capable agent may use
a lawful refinement sequence. Both preserve the same material invariants and
authority relations.

## Frame Qualification

A candidate frame is qualified proportionally to its claims. The minimum
evaluation asks:

1. **Decision test**: Can a competent actor produce a result in the declared
   algebra for representative in-domain evaluations?
2. **Reconstruction test**: Can a fresh competent actor activate it from the
   declared packet without hidden history?
3. **Sensitivity test**: Do changes to declared material relations affect the
   result where expected?
4. **Irrelevance test**: Do excluded unrelated changes leave the result stable
   where expected?
5. **Basis test**: Does stale, mismatched, or ambiguous basis information
   invalidate or refuse the evaluation?
6. **Authority test**: Does frame selection preserve semantic and decision
   authority and the exact inherited operation authority for every material
   construction, transformation, admission, publication, or mutation?
7. **Counterexample test**: Does the frame detect representative historical or
   constructed violations it claims to cover?
8. **Capability test**: Can the declared actor class hold and operate the frame
   within its envelope?
9. **Result-consumption test**: Can a declared consumer use the closed result
   without importing hidden frame context?

Qualification binds the exact frame revision, actor envelope, evaluation
family, basis class, and evidence conditions evaluated. It does not generalize
to an unevaluated population by naming the frame generically.

Apply [Workspace and context](FOUNDATIONS.md#workspace-and-context) when reusing
that qualification after a source, task, actor or dependency change. Its
capability evidence covers only the measured or supported envelope; successful
task use does not measure unknown host context. Conversely, a byte count alone
does not establish adequate attention to the selected task. These are separate
claims under the same bound qualification, not interchangeable pass signals.

### Method Self-Qualification

Qualification of this method uses the constructor, freeze, assessor,
acceptance, and release protocols supplied by its external governing
authority; it does not define those protocols. A fresh competent constructor
operates inside a declared capability, context, and configuration envelope
using this method and declared trial subjects. An independently authorized
evaluator then compares the exact stabilized results with this method's
complete mandatory relation population.

The qualification population includes every function and failure relation the
method claims:

| Relation under qualification | Required representative observations |
|---|---|
| `declare` | a frame set derived from a governed workspace, outcome, practices, capability envelopes, evaluation inventory, authority, and residual boundary; missing authority or evaluation basis refuses |
| `activate` | exact subject/basis and capable-actor activation succeeds; stale, ambiguous, topology-mismatched, unauthorized, and capability-incompatible activations refuse or return the applicable closed pressure |
| `evaluate` | representative `satisfied`, `falsified`, `indeterminate`, `out_of_frame`, and `invalid_basis` results; sensitivity to material changes and stability under established irrelevance |
| `conjoin` | exercise [core conjunction](FOUNDATIONS.md#result-conjunction): all required claims and joins satisfied; an authenticated applicable failure retained beside missing coverage; invalid composite or required basis; required out-of-frame contribution; and unresolved mandatory contribution. Conflict, veto and refusal remain predicate-bound evidence or decision annotations, never replacement verdicts; preserve the core precedence and every frame's meaning |
| `translate` | preserved coordinates and provenance reach the target; declared loss remains explicit; identity, basis, authority, or equality mismatch refuses |
| `audit_coverage` | evaluation, interaction, semantic/operation/decision authority, failure, capability, and required independently activated or assurance-independent coverage map to capable frames or explicit residuals; artifact or actor count cannot close coverage |
| `revise` | historical failure, topology mutation, evidence change, and capability change produce the declared frame-set delta and identify every prior activation, result, conjunction, and coverage claim requiring reevaluation |
| result consumption | a declared consumer uses one closed result without importing hidden frame context or authority; insufficient output produces a reconstructability failure |
| material composition | a case requiring cross-frame execution declares composition, order where material, closure, failure, and semantic/operation/decision-authority conservation; a case not requiring composition remains uncomposed |

One valid frame-set declaration and coverage result establishes only the
`declare` and evaluated `audit_coverage` population. It cannot prove activation,
evaluation, conjunction, translation, revision, result consumption, or material
composition by implication.

Each relation is exercised through representative positive, boundary, and
falsifying cases. Qualification may be staged, but every verdict states the
exact functions, frame revisions, actor envelopes, basis classes, result
branches, and evidence populations evaluated. An unevaluated function or branch
remains open rather than inheriting a whole-method verdict.

Prior exemplar frame sets or outcomes may be used as optional comparison
evidence after freeze. They are not mandatory target answers unless the
governing basis explicitly requires equivalence.

## Anti-Patterns

The following are method violations where they support a frame or coverage
claim:

1. one omniscient frame is presumed to contain the complete method or
   workspace;
2. a module, file, service, role, persona, prompt, or lens is called a frame
   without a declared evaluation relation;
3. functional decomposition, authority hierarchy, and frame topology are
   silently treated as one tree;
4. every active frame is required to compose with every other frame;
5. composition machinery is introduced without a material cross-frame
   evaluation;
6. frame selection or broad visibility is treated as semantic authority;
7. evidence is flattened across bases or coordinates and treated as one
   executed or observed relation;
8. `satisfied` is returned where the lawful result is `indeterminate`,
   `out_of_frame`, or `invalid_basis`;
9. coverage is inferred from artifact, file, operation, test, review, or actor
   count;
10. a frame silently grows beyond the selected actor's capability envelope;
11. a topology mutation reuses a stale activation without recharting;
12. every failure creates another frame before activation, basis, evidence,
    capability, and evaluation defects are excluded;
13. intentional overlap is contracted even though it supplies independently
    activated failure detection or materially different capability fit; or
14. a generic runtime, registry, controller, or universal carrier is presented
    as required by this method.

## Closure

A project operates under this method when:

1. human authority has declared the governed workspace, outcome, evaluation
   inventory, and frame set;
2. every active frame is finite, evaluation-bound, basis-bound, authority-safe,
   and usable inside its actor capability envelope;
3. frame results preserve semantic, evidence, inference, and decision
   distinctions;
4. cross-frame conjunction, translation, or composition exists only where a
   material evaluation requires it;
5. workspace coverage maps every known material evaluation and interaction to
   a capable frame or explicit residual;
6. operational full coverage is stated relative to its exact tuple;
7. topology, capability, evidence, and historical failures can trigger frame
   revision or recharting; and
8. no executable frame machinery, global context, universal completeness, or
   domain authority is inferred from method adoption.
