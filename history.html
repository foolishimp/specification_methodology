The continuing question

Preserving meaning
through change.

STDO (Specification, Ticketing, Design, and Outcome-Driven Development) developed through applications, coding agents, development workflows, execution engines and a parallel investigation into constraints and emergence. Its independent repository began in April 2026. The author dates the beginning of the work to mid-2024, before the surviving application check-ins. Its practical ancestry includes a collaborative whiteboard, Chain of Thoth and Coder for Hire, usually shortened to C4H.

The applications and the foundational investigation developed together. In the author’s account, questions about the fundamentals were already part of the early work. The later Constraint-Emergence Ontology captured that thinking in a more explicit form. Its repository creation date therefore marks a publication and separation of the work, rather than the beginning of the inquiry.

Across these projects, the recurring problem was how to turn an intention into an implementation while preserving the relationships that made the intention meaningful. Each generation made another part of that problem explicit: state and history, task context, transformation lineage, requirements, evaluation, authority, recovery and the limits of a completion claim.

A historical account, dated 21 November 2026The latest published method cut covered here is STDO 3.1.0-rc.3, published on 18 November. The account describes the lineage; each consumer’s selected immutable release governs its rules.

Mid-2024

2024: applications, state and the first construction agents

State, relationships and a history of change.

Mid-2024: the work begins

The author’s initial work began in mid-2024, using conversational AI to explore ideas and construct applications. The first application recalled was a Mural replacement, later preserved as Brain Dump / MyPostit. The surviving Git history anchors the experiments in August and the whiteboard implementation in September; it does not establish the first day of the work.

The model releases provide context for this period. ChatGPT launched using GPT-3.5 on 30 November 2022, and Anthropic introduced Claude on 14 March 2023. By mid-2024, OpenAI had announced GPT-4o on 13 May, and Anthropic released Claude 3.5 Sonnet in June. The project’s mid-2024 start therefore belongs to that later generation of tools. It is separate from the original launches of ChatGPT and Claude. These dates describe availability; they do not establish which model was used for each early experiment. ChatGPT introduction, Claude introduction, GPT-4o announcement, Claude 3.5 Sonnet

Brain Dump and MyPostit

The likely Mural replacement is the project preserved as Jims-Brain-Dump and MyPostit. Both repositories share the same initial history. A scaffold was checked in on 15 September 2024, followed that day by the first substantial TypeScript application, commit b361df7605.

The application had an infinite canvas, movable notes, arrows and connection points, storage, import and export, and an event history interface. It was already an exercise in keeping relationships intact as objects changed. Moving a note also meant preserving the meaning and position of its connections. The event-log and undo/redo machinery show an early concern with recoverable state, although their presence alone does not establish complete replay correctness. First application check-in

The following commits record the experience of building with an AI assistant more candidly than a retrospective feature list could. They describe lost arrows, unstable positioning and a Claude 3.5 refactor. The record supports a limited but useful observation: apparently local changes could damage relationships elsewhere in the application. Later concerns with preserved meaning and evidence can be understood against that experience. This is a historical interpretation, rather than a claim that the September application already contained STDO’s later theory.

An earlier side branch, discover_build, was checked in on 20 August 2024. It combined retrieval using FAISS and sentence embeddings with AutoGen agents for topic refinement and code execution. It establishes that experimentation with model context and agent-mediated work preceded the surviving whiteboard implementation. A direct derivation from this experiment to C4H has not been established. Retrieval and agent experiment

Chain of Thoth: making change explicit

Chain of Thoth begins on 17 September 2024. Its early commits explicitly introduce command/query separation and event sourcing. The Rust backend represents notes, arrows, groups and embedded boards, with command handlers, query handlers, an event store, snapshots and collaborative sessions. React and Flutter clients followed in September and October. Chain of Thoth source

This made the application a system with an explicit history of change. A board was a state reconstructed and communicated through operations, with separate paths for changing it and observing it. That distinction later reappeared in the separation between runtime facts and their projections, although the repositories do not establish a continuous reuse of the same code.

The techniques themselves were established. Event sourcing, including reconstruction from recorded changes, had been described well before 2024. The significance here is their adoption in the author’s early AI-assisted application work. The later methodology grew partly from problems exposed by using these techniques. Fowler’s 2005 account of event sourcing

From a board to a Unit of Thought

The connection to the coding-agent work becomes explicit in refactorer, whose first check-in is dated 20 October UTC, or 21 October in Sydney. Its implementation uses specialized AutoGen agents for intent, scoping, analysis, refactoring and testing. A coder4h_config.yaml file places the Coder for Hire name in this precursor.

More revealing is Chain_of_Thoth_v001.md, retained in the refactorer repository. It describes a Unit of Thought with operations for capturing intent, constraining scope, verifying and optimizing. Feedback can travel back through the structure. Agent specializations include discovery, scoping, refactoring, testing and Coder4H. The document is a direct bridge between the Thoth name and the construction-agent design. Unit of Thought design

The conceptual unit had changed. A note on a board was a piece of represented thought; the proposed Unit of Thought was a bounded piece of work with intent, constraints, execution and feedback. It anticipated later concerns with construction units and evaluation, while leaving much of their meaning to the agent implementation.

This work belonged to an active field. Microsoft’s September 2023 AutoGen announcement already described specialized agents combining models, tools and human participation. MetaGPT, first submitted in August 2023, encoded software-team roles and operating procedures into agent workflows. SWE-agent’s May 2024 paper examined interfaces through which agents navigate repositories, edit code and execute tests. The presence of several agents or a testing role was therefore established practice by the time of refactorer. AutoGen, MetaGPT, SWE-agent

The distinctive direction within this lineage was the attempt to make intent, scope and feedback properties of the work unit itself. That is a design contribution visible in the source, without implying that the project had demonstrated superior performance or invented multi-agent development.

Late 2024–2025

Late 2024-2025: C4H and the preservation of intent

C4H makes work orders, context and transformation lineage explicit.

C4H was a sequence of implementations

The surviving c4h_intent_system history begins on 3 November 2024. Its architecture describes an intent-driven flow through discovery, solution, refactoring and verification. Events carry operation lineage, including root, parent and branch relationships. Deterministic skills and model-driven work occupy different roles. Early C4H repository

The implementation changed quickly. November commits record a move away from an AutoGen 0.4 attempt toward LiteLLM, followed by semantic extraction, iteration, coding and feedback work. A Prefect implementation appears in December. The main c4h repository starts on 31 December with a commit that explicitly says it reinitializes a monorepo. That date marks a consolidation; the earlier repositories preserve its antecedents. The author’s recollection places the beginnings of C4H around mid-2024, but the named implementation records recovered here establish October and November as firm anchors. C4H consolidation

During early 2025, C4H separated agent capabilities from service orchestration. Discovery, solution and coding could be arranged into configured teams. Execution context carried a workflow identifier, an agent execution identifier, a parent and an execution path. The editor provided a place to submit and inspect work orders. These changes addressed practical questions: which request produced a change, which agent acted, and which context it used.

The May methodology documents made that separation more explicit. They distinguished the interface, the declarative methodology and the reference implementation. Configuration snapshots, stateless agents, explicit responses and traceable transformations were recurring concerns. The SDLC account connected intent and work orders to discovery, design, coding and assurance. C4H methodology and process archive

The innovation within C4H was this combination of work-order semantics, configured transformation stages and execution lineage. Each ingredient had precedents. Their combination made the system an experiment in accountable construction across several steps. Recording inputs and configuration improved inspectability; it did not by itself make probabilistic outputs deterministic or establish that every transformation preserved meaning.

The foundational inquiry was already present

The C4H archive contains From Constrained Problem Domains to Emergent Reasoning in LLMs, first checked in on 22 February 2025. Following the file across later moves is necessary to recover that date. Its eventual location under Philosophy/ can otherwise make it appear younger. First preserved reasoning paper

The paper asks how constraints reduce a problem’s possible solutions, how attention might be understood as a dynamic constraint mechanism, and whether the matching performed by a transformer has a useful relationship to symbolic unification. It also considers statistical boundaries and symbol-like representations. These were proposed interpretations of computation. They supplied questions and candidate models for the engineering work, rather than a measured guarantee about model behavior.

A second bridge appears in A Formal Model for Semantic Transformation in AI-Augmented Processes, checked in on 18 July 2025. It describes a chain from root or regulatory intent, through contextual policy and business requirements, to architecture and a solution. Transformations should add specificity while preserving the source’s meaning. Where several constraints meet, the document explores compositional reconciliation using category-theoretic language. Semantic transformation model

This is an identifiable ancestor of STDO’s later authority chain. It moves attention from the sequence of agents to the relationship between artifacts: what permits a downstream decision, what it must preserve, and how it can be traced back. The paper’s language sometimes presents semantic preservation as a guarantee of the proposed model. The historical record establishes the proposal; it does not establish a general proof that an LLM implementation satisfies it.

The emergent_reasoning repository began on 30 July 2025. Like the later ontology repository, it separated work already present elsewhere. The practical and theoretical branches were now independently visible, while continuing to inform one another. Emergent Reasoning first check-in

August 2025–January 2026

August 2025-January 2026: development practice becomes a methodology

Development practices become a subject of development in their own right.

The August projects claude_init, claude_dev and ai_init extracted reusable development practices from individual applications. They supplied project instructions, task records, test-first practices and session continuity. ai_init explicitly began as a copy of claude_init. Development bootstrap, AI bootstrap

ai_sdlc_method starts on 15 October 2025 as a configuration and context management system. The following day’s history explicitly integrates ai_init as its baseline development practices. This is a documented transfer, rather than an inferred resemblance between two repositories. AI SDLC origin, Baseline integration

Through November, the project developed requirements, design, code and testing skills, configurable context and implementations for different coding-agent environments. The method could increasingly be described separately from a particular agent or application. C4H had implemented a development process; AI SDLC was making the process itself a reusable subject of development.

By this time, specification-led agent workflows were also becoming a visible part of the wider tooling landscape. Kiro’s 14 July 2025 introduction described requirements, technical design and implementation tasks, with acceptance criteria and links back to requirements. GitHub’s 2 September 2025 Spec Kit announcement described specification, planning, tasks and implementation across several coding agents, with human checkpoints. Kiro’s introduction, Spec Kit’s introduction

These are relevant contemporaries, not evidence of influence in either direction. They establish that documents before code, task decomposition, traceability and multiple supported agents were shared concerns by late 2025. The distinctive thread developing here was the connection to a proposed model of semantic refinement: the method was intended to explain what survives a transformation, as well as to organize the work of performing it.

January–February 2026

January-February 2026: the Constraint-Emergence Ontology

A parallel inquiry into persistence, boundaries and meaning.

The ontology was first checked into AI SDLC on 22 January 2026, in commit 3760f2fae0. Its initial synthesis connected questions about physics, computation and emergence with requirements and homeostasis. The separate constraint_emergence_ontology repository began on 9 February 2026; AI SDLC then recorded the migration of the philosophy material out of its own tree. Ontology synthesis, Independent repository

The inquiry asked what allows structures to form, persist, interact and become components of further construction. Constraints described admissible states and changes. Boundaries allowed a structure to be treated as a unit. Meaning was considered through relationships that survive transformation. Evaluation and feedback connected a model of an intended state to observations of an actual state.

For software, this suggested a practical interpretation. A requirement could act as a continuing condition on a product. An implementation was one realization of those conditions. Observations could reveal a gap in the implementation, the design, or the requirements themselves. Repair therefore needed a way to return to the layer responsible for the discrepancy.

The ontology gave this interpretation a vocabulary and a broader research programme. Its physical and cross-domain claims remained hypotheses or philosophical proposals with their own evidential burdens. The engineering method could use explicit constraints, boundaries and feedback without requiring those broader claims to be established.

The later revisions matter to the history. The September 2026 working paper distinguishes constraints, dynamics and evaluation. It requires an account of reachability and persistence before inferring that an allowed structure will form. It reserves a Markov-boundary claim for a declared screening property that has been tested. Its research discussion reports structured representations and interventions alongside failed screening tests. Constraint and Emergence, September revision

This is a refinement of the original programme. The language became more careful about the difference between a useful organizing model and an established mechanism. In the method, the corresponding discipline is to state what a claim is about, what would count as evidence, and where the claim stops applying. The source record supports that correspondence; it does not establish that every later STDO rule was deduced from the ontology.

February–March 2026

February-March 2026: Genesis and the asset graph

Construction and evaluation become a shared operation across artifacts.

On 19 February 2026, AI SDLC records the Asset Graph Model. The Genesis name appears in the same repository later that week. The transformation was conceptual as well as nominal: a fixed development sequence was recast in terms of four primitives—assets in a graph, iteration, evaluators, and specification plus context. Asset graph revision

The central operation was written as:

iterate(Asset, Context[], Evaluators) → Asset'

An intent, a design, a piece of code or a test artifact could be treated as a subject of construction and evaluation. A software lifecycle was one configuration of the graph. Different work could select different assets, transitions, evaluators and stopping conditions. Human judgment, probabilistic model work and deterministic evaluation became explicit categories, later written as F_H, F_P and F_D.

The ontology connection was declared in the project’s README. Requirements and context bounded construction; evaluation determined whether a candidate met the selected conditions; telemetry and other observations could reopen work. The February Genesis Bootloader attempted to express the governing ideas in a compact context that an LLM could consume. The associated abiogenesis principle proposed human bootstrapping followed by a system capable of sustaining its own development. Genesis account, Bootloader, Abiogenesis principle

Graphs and evaluation were already part of AI tooling. DSPy’s October 2023 paper described declarative language-model modules in transformation graphs, optimized against a metric. LangGraph’s January 2024 introduction supplied cyclic graphs for agent runtimes. Genesis’s graph representation therefore belongs to a wider development in the field. Its particular synthesis applied a common construction-and-evaluation model across lifecycle artifacts, with the specification/context boundary carrying the conditions of acceptable work. DSPy, LangGraph

One March decision makes the intended freedom of realization explicit. ADR-S-036, dated 12 March and revised on 14 March, treated invariants as conditions to satisfy, with room for agents of different capabilities to take different construction paths. It referred to a project built in a construction pass followed by an assurance pass. That account motivated the decision; it is not independent qualification of the reported build. Invariant and procedure decision

The decision also exposes a tension that later versions had to resolve. Equivalent final artifacts do not settle every question about authorized effects, necessary causal order or admissible evidence. STDO would retain freedom where the contract permits it while making those additional obligations explicit. The progression was a continuing adjustment of what belongs in the method and what belongs in a particular realization.

March–June 2026

March-June 2026: separating engine, lifecycle and method

Engine, software lifecycle and general lifecycle acquire distinct owners.

ABIogenesis and genesis_sdlc

ABIogenesis begins on 15 March 2026 with an explicit statement that the earlier Genesis engine had accumulated implementation history and needed a clean reconstruction. Its initial intent names six functions—iterate, project, emit, bind_fd, delta and schedule—and a GTL-first engine. GTL, the graph declaration language, was being developed as a typed Python library. The initial plan set a self-hosting gate in which the engine would build a successor of itself. ABIogenesis initial intent

The compiler analogy was deliberate. A language describes the construction; an engine executes it; the engine can eventually be built using an earlier engine. The first intent even proposed that the GTL package itself was the requirements surface. Later work gave requirements and design their own authority again. This records an actual change of position about how much meaning should be owned by an executable declaration.

genesis_sdlc also begins on 15 March. It supplied the software-development graph and practices that a generic engine would not own. Its subsequent specification describes that missing layer through a compiler-and-standard- library analogy. Work on module decomposition addressed a concrete weakness: a direct jump from design to code forced module-boundary decisions and implementation decisions into the same step. genesis_sdlc origin and specification

The separation made several different questions visible. The engine needed to execute and preserve runtime facts. The software lifecycle needed to say what those facts meant for development. The method needed to say what counted as an authorized and sufficiently evidenced result. Sharing a vocabulary did not make those responsibilities interchangeable.

odd_sdlc: retaining selected foundations

The repository now called odd_sdlc begins on 5 April 2026, initially using the odd_method name. Its first intent explicitly describes a fresh constitutional line informed by genesis_sdlc. It retained specification authority, graph functions, GTL/ABIogenesis execution, runtime fact ownership and scenario-based evidence. It rejected the inheritance of a product-local automation layer that duplicated runtime responsibility. odd_sdlc founding intent

This was selective re-adoption. A successor was expected to identify which predecessor requirements it still wanted and where they were now owned. Repository ancestry and working code were insufficient reasons to preserve every old obligation or implementation choice. Later STDO rules about migration and predecessor conservation gave this lesson a general form.

odd_glc: the lifecycle extends beyond software

On 27 June 2026, odd_glc, the ODD General Life Cycle, received its first check-in. Its bootstrap cites the preceding ABIogenesis requirements- algebra strategy and observations from odd_sdlc. It identifies concerns such as materialization, evidence, partial completion, unresolved obligations, re-entry and release as applicable beyond software delivery. General lifecycle bootstrap

Its intended position was between the generic execution substrate and domain products. ABIogenesis/GTL would preserve and execute the declared structures; odd_glc would interpret their lifecycle meaning; domain products would add their own policy and interpretation. The bootstrap expressly prevents the new layer from becoming another local runtime or closure ledger.

The general-lifecycle branch is recorded as odd_glc, distinct from the software-development specialization odd_sdlc.

March–April 2026

March-April 2026: Specification Methodology becomes independent

Shared standards acquire their own source and release identity.

The method predates its repository. SPEC_METHOD.md was first checked into genesis_sdlc on 27 March 2026, with an internal document date of 24 March. GRAPH_METHOD.md followed on 3 April. ABIogenesis distributed copies into installed workspaces. That arrangement made a shared method depend on which product’s source tree happened to supply the installer. First specification method, First graph method

The independent specification_methodology repository was established on 7 April 2026, commit 287c866787. Its provenance document states the reason: remove ambiguity over the authoritative source. Shared standards would be authored in one place, published as complete immutable cuts, and adopted through exact release identities. Installed copies would derive their authority from the selected cut. Independent method origin

The extracted method made the direction of authority explicit. Intent and requirements govern design; requirements and design govern realization. Evidence can reveal a defect at any of those layers. A report, index or generated view remains a representation of its source. The process for returning to an owning layer allows the specification to evolve without allowing a local workaround to rewrite it silently.

This diagram summarizes the authority and feedback relationship. It is not a mandatory runtime schedule. Some work can satisfy several obligations in one construction, while a declared causal or evidence obligation may require a particular ordering.

The independent repository is the point at which lessons from the engines became a separately versioned method. Engine behavior could now be evaluated against it. A working engine would no longer define the method solely through its existing behavior.

April–June 2026

April-June 2026: the first released method

The product remains answerable to its conditions after construction.

The 1.x line established a method that could govern its own development and the products using it. It distinguished a mutable source project, an immutable release, an installed product and a product acting as the builder of another product. This distinction followed directly from the bootstrap experience: the source being edited and the installed tool doing the work could have different identities and different authority.

The line also accumulated rules for migration, bounded work, design, probabilistic operations and evaluation. Each addition addressed a different way that useful construction could lose its connection to the intended outcome. A sprint needed a bounded claim. A migration needed to say which old requirements survived. A successful test needed to be related to the property it actually observed.

By 1.8.0, the scope explicitly covered the operational lifecycle, including release, deployment, live use, telemetry and retirement. Correctness had become a continuing relationship between the specification and the observed product. This carried forward the homeostatic interpretation developed in Genesis and the ontology: a system remains answerable to its governing conditions as its environment and implementation change.

July–September 2026

July-September 2026: STDO 2 and the boundaries of evidence

Every result needs a subject, a basis and a stated limit.

An incremental successor with stricter claims

2.0.0, in July, explicitly conserved the accepted 1.8.0 method. Its release note describes an incremental successor and distinguishes it from earlier rejected reconstruction candidates. The method added design-boundary work and lessons from delivery incidents. 2.0.0 release account

Several of those lessons concern the difference between a useful observation and the claim someone wants it to support. Evidence must identify its subject. A test through a substitute path does not establish behavior on the actual authority path. A result for one candidate does not automatically qualify a changed candidate. Passing a component check does not close a product-level outcome. Independent review must have an identifiable subject and a durable disposition.

This sharpened the earlier lineage work. C4H had asked which operation produced an output. STDO now also asked what that output justified, under which basis, for which subject, and with what remaining uncertainty.

The method also made its own cost part of the judgment. A disproportionate control or redesign could damage delivery without providing evidence relevant to the actual claim. The developing position required sufficient structure and assurance for the consequence at issue. It did not equate more ceremony with a stronger result.

The ontology also had to respect its application boundaries

The 2.2.2 release records a specific correction to the world-model method. Earlier wording had made a geometric Markov-object construction part of the publication requirements for recovered entities. That could reject an institutional or software entity whose identity and evidence were adequate but whose source offered no suitable geometric representation. Entity and geometry correction

The correction made the entity cut the primary semantic unit and treated the geometric Markov-object account as an optional overlay requiring an appropriate representation space. This is a concrete example of the foundational inquiry being refined through use. An explanatory model had introduced an obligation that did not fit every governed domain. The method retained the useful boundary questions and narrowed the geometric claim.

Reference frames and a shared result vocabulary

The August 2.4.0 line introduced a general reference-frame account. A judgment has a purpose, a subject, a governing basis, an authority boundary and evidence. A result can be satisfied, falsified, indeterminate, outside the frame, or based on an invalid basis. These distinctions preserve information that a single success/failure flag loses. Reference-frame release

For example, an evaluator lacking the necessary observation has a different result from an evaluator that observed a counterexample. An out-of-scope judgment cannot be made relevant by reporting confidence. A valid result about an earlier release does not silently transfer to a later one. These were methodological conditions for interpreting results, with concrete realization left to the applicable product.

The 2.4.x line also established the STDO naming and Product Definition binding, and made release selection and installation more explicit. The 2.5.x work developed a shared axiomatic account, source-linked representations and construction/evaluation profiles. Deterministic, probabilistic and human functions remained distinguishable from the actors performing them and from the authority to accept their results.

October–November 2026

October-November 2026: STDO 3, finite context and separate products

STDO 3 separates product responsibilities and strengthens continuation.

Reconstructing accumulated rules around common foundations

The October work developed separate Claude and Codex constructions against a common specification, followed by comparison and synthesis. The common definition owned the required meaning; each tenant supplied a realization. Candidate packaging and comparison results retained their own scope. Neither the existence of two implementations nor a successful package check established whole-method qualification.

Version 3 organized the accumulated method around shared foundations. Entities, relations, authority, selected bases and evaluation results had clearer common owners. Reference frames and independent review already existed in 2.x; the new work integrated them and made finite context, activation and continuation more explicit. V3 source project and construction record

This addressed a recurring operational fact of AI-assisted development: a session is finite. A later actor may receive only part of the earlier context. A useful continuation therefore needs enough current state to identify the subject, its governing source, completed work, unresolved matters and the next authorized action. A summary can direct attention, but its omissions cannot amend the underlying obligations.

The method’s result vocabulary also became a common basis for composing judgments. Missing coverage, an invalid basis and a demonstrated failure retain their different meanings when work is combined. A coordinator consumes results within its grant; a writer needs authority for its effects; applicable review requires the necessary independence. A particular Executive/Worker/Reviewer arrangement remains a selected profile rather than a universal organizational chart.

From a method package to independently owned supporting products

The first native 3.0.0 candidate cut was recorded in October. Subsequent releases developed distribution, recursive product composition, tools and native workflows. By November, the repository also contained independently owned support products. Their co-location did not give one product authority over another.

3.0.4 supplied native work, review, status, ticket and help workflows. It made distinctions such as landing a change, closing work and qualifying a release visible in ordinary use. It later remained the construction basis for the 3.1 release work. A methodology can therefore be built under one released version while producing a different successor.

The 3.1 arrangement separated Purpose Context, which owns reusable indexes and context construction, from STDO Skills, which binds those capabilities to supported hosts. Representation Tools and STDO Representation serve visual documentation. Axiom Indexer retains its separately owned conversion and projection responsibilities. This was another application of the ownership lesson learned during the engine/lifecycle split.

The history of representation tools includes formal and Lean-related work. The 3.1 boundary changes retired the earlier formal/context routes from the active product arrangement and made visual documentation optional. An index or diagram could help a reader find and understand a rule while remaining answerable to its exact source. It would not become a new owner of that rule.

RC3: preserving obligations while removing an inherited mechanism

The latest published cut in this account is specification_methodology/v3.1.0-rc.3, native commit b2e21ad0a2bab04e59afd5246b990da992417047. GitHub records its publication on 18 November 2026 at 08:19:51 UTC. The project published it as a normal GitHub release while retaining the rc.3 identity. Those are separate facts; the label should not be silently rewritten as a final 3.1.0 cut. Published release

RC3 corrected a remaining dependency of method meaning on downstream products and runtime machinery. Every normative tier, including optional profiles, must define its terms and rules within the method. Tracking obligations, decisions, progress and unresolved matters remains required. So does conserving needed facts and reconciling a work record with the actual workspace. A generic register is one possible realization. ODD no longer imposes an immutable ledger, a fixed evaluator API or a universal replay architecture. RC3 native release note

That correction is historically significant. Event histories and runtime lineage had been useful from the first whiteboard and C4H implementations. They helped expose the need to preserve state, explain change and retain evidence. By RC3, the method stated those obligations without requiring every consumer to reproduce the machinery through which they had first been explored. A product may still require replay or a particular ledger when its own contract calls for them.

RC3 also clarified that Product describes the intended product and end state, including capabilities still to be built, while Goals describes the current epics or work wave. That distinction prevents a bounded delivery plan from quietly shrinking the product’s intended outcome.

The release retains six independent products and the STDO 3.0.4 construction basis. Its evidence distinguishes source assessment, package identity, context coverage, installed-host observations and public acquisition. The release note explicitly limits the conclusions: publication does not establish whole-method qualification, universal context coverage or successful future tasks, and it does not migrate existing consumers. Later mutable source work also remains a separate subject from this historical release boundary. RC3 distribution and evidence record

What accumulated

The contribution that accumulated

Preservation, evaluation, authority and correction across generations.

The differentiators are most accurately described as a combination that developed over time. The early projects used established application and agent techniques. The later work made their governing relationships an independent object of design.

Meaning across transformations. C4H’s intent and operation lineage, the 2025 semantic model and STDO’s requirements/design chain all address preservation through change. The refinement was to make preservation an explicit obligation with a subject and evidence, rather than assume it from the fact that several stages completed.

A shared account of construction and evaluation. Genesis applied its asset-and-evaluator model to multiple development artifacts. STDO generalized the conditions of acceptable construction while allowing different products to choose their realizations. This extends the project’s scope beyond a particular coding-agent loop or document-to-task workflow.

Authority and evidence that remain attached to their basis. A source document, released method, installed tool, generated view and evaluation result have different roles. The separation became especially important when the method was used to build itself and when several products and coding agents shared a workspace.

Correction at the layer that owns the problem. A failed observation can require a code repair, a different design, a revised requirement or a changed goal. The lifecycle provides a way to make that decision explicit. Partial completion and unresolved obligations remain visible instead of disappearing inside a broad success claim.

Fundamental ideas exposed to engineering limits. The constraint-emergence work supplied a way of thinking about boundaries, persistence, meaning and feedback. Application and research then exposed where the account needed qualification: geometric representations were not available everywhere; screening claims needed tests; admissible outcomes did not remove authority or causal obligations; useful runtime patterns could become over-prescriptive when promoted into general method.

These are claims about the design and documented evolution of this project. The contemporary sources establish that several component ideas were already in use. They do not support a claim of sole invention, industry-wide priority or comparative performance. The distinctive work lies in the particular integration, its continued revision through application, and the effort to state exactly which relationships an alternative realization must preserve.

Sources and remaining historical gaps

Check-ins, releases, contemporary publications and the remaining gaps.

This account uses surviving Git histories, initial intents, design documents, method release notes and the public release record. The whiteboard’s intended role as a Mural replacement, the approximate mid-2024 recollection of C4H, and the presence of foundational thinking during the earliest work are attributed to the author’s recollection. Those recollections help identify the projects; a later preserved check-in does not establish an earlier date.

Three distinctions matter when extending this history. First, a file move or repository extraction can conceal an older first check-in; following the reasoning paper across its moves recovered February 2025. Second, a commit message reporting successful tests or a completed build records the author’s assessment at that time; this history has not rerun and independently qualified those implementations. Third, conceptual influence and direct code descent are different relationships. The narrative identifies an explicit transfer where the source provides one and labels retrospective interpretation where it does not.

The comparison with other tools uses their dated publications. It concerns what those sources described at the time, rather than claiming to survey every capability of their current versions.
